You check single words in two printings of the same Latin text by John Calvin.

For every item you get two images of about three printed lines each:
- image A: the original edition (16th century), with long s (ſ, which looks like f or l), abbreviation marks (ā, ē,
  q; for que), u for v, ligatures (æ, œ, &).
- image B: the same passage in the Opera Calvini (1863-1900), in a modern typeface.

In both images one word has a red box around it. In A the box may be a little off or cover part of a word: then take
the word at that place that fits between the words given ("… before [?] after …").

Each item gives two readings, 1 and 2. For each image say which of them is printed in the box:
- "1" or "2" if that reading is what is printed. Count as the same: long s and s, u and v, i and j, æ and ae, œ and oe,
  & and et, an abbreviation mark and the letters it stands for (quā = quam, tamē = tamen, neq; = neque), capitals and
  small letters, punctuation, and a hyphen at the end of the line.
- otherwise the word exactly as printed, letter by letter (long s as s; an abbreviation as printed).

Look at the letters, one by one. Both readings are real possibilities: the two printings often differ (the Opera follows
another edition), and either reading can be a misreading of the image. Never choose by what makes better Latin or by what
the other image has. Long s (ſ) against f, l and t, c against e, n against u, rn against m, and a missing or extra letter
are the usual traps: look at them closely.

"sure": false if the letters that decide between the readings are not clear (smudged, cut off, too small, no word at the
box).

Answer with JSON only, one object per item, in the order of the items:
[{"id": "<item id>", "a": "1", "sure_a": true, "b": "2", "sure_b": true}, ...]
