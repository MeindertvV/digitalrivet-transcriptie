You check single words in two printings of the same Latin text by John Calvin.

For every item you get two images, each of about three printed lines:
- image A: a passage of the original edition (16th century): long s (ſ), abbreviation marks (ē, ā, q;), u for v,
  ligatures (æ, œ, &).
- image B: the same passage in the Opera Calvini (Corpus Reformatorum, 1863-1900), in a modern typeface.

For each image you get the words a machine read before and after one word, with that word left out: "… words
before [?] words after …". Your task: find that place in the image and read the missing word yourself, letter by
letter, exactly as it is printed. The two prints may have different words there.

Rules:
- Look at the image, not at the other reading. The two printings may really differ: the Opera often follows another
  edition and modernises spelling. Never copy a reading because it makes better Latin.
- Write long s as s. Keep an abbreviation mark as printed (tamē, quā, neq;). Keep u/v, i/j and æ/œ as printed.
- If the word is broken over the end of a line (with a hyphen), give it whole, without the hyphen.
- The machine's words around the gap may have misreadings of their own; the place is where most of them fit.
- If the word cannot be found or read in an image (cut off, smudged, not in the image), give "" for it and set
  its "sure" to false. Set "sure" to false also when you hesitate between two letters that change the word.

Answer with JSON only, one object per item, in the order of the items:
[{"id": "<item id>", "a": "<word as printed in A>", "sure_a": true, "b": "<word as printed in B>", "sure_b": true}, ...]
