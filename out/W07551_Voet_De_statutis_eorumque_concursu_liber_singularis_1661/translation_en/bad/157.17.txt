[1] 6. But when a person becomes an adult by virtue of a statute, does the time for seeking restitution in integrum begin to run for him at once, or only after the age fixed by civil law, that is, after the 25th year? I answer that the time for him to seek restitution does run from the year after 18 or 20, as the statute has laid down, but it continues until the time that civil law grants to minors for restitution. This is so for several reasons. First, a person who has obtained a grant of age from the Prince can, according to the doctors, be restored even after four years from the grant, even after the judge has taken cognizance and examined the matter, provided he is still a minor under 25. If this holds for a rescript of the Prince granting age, it will hold much more for a statute, which achieves no more than a grant of age, and which may introduce a lesser correction of civil law, or at least no greater one, than the rescript itself. Second, statutes are to be explained and moderated so that they do not contain a case of double correction of civil law. That would happen if, besides the correction of the minor's age, a correction of the time for restitution in integrum were also added. Third, what was introduced by statute for the benefit of minors, so that they might be freed more quickly from guardianship and obtain the management of their own affairs, ought not to be turned to their hurt. <i>l. 5. in sin. init.
