[1] 4. And this is enough on the definition itself. Next comes the question: who can introduce privileges? Anyone who can make a general law or right can introduce one. So an emperor within his empire, a king within his kingdom, a prince within his principality, and a free commonwealth within its own territory, so far as each can make law. Indeed, whoever can make some right in his own way can also grant some privilege. And in the same way that a right is introduced, a privilege will be introduced too, for instance by custom or by prescription. For all things bound by a given law are also loosed by that same law, and whoever has the power to grant an action has the power to deny it, to absolve has the power to condemn, and to abrogate laws has the power to enact them; for one who may do the greater thing may not be forbidden the lesser. <i>l. 53. l. 100.
