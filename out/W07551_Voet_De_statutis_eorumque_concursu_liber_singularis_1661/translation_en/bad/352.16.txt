[1] 6. But I shall return to where I had digressed, to the conflict of statutes that differ with respect to judicial proceedings. Here several matters arise concerning the formalities to be observed in courts: concerning time limits, security, proofs, the decision of cases, executions, and appeals. For suppose that different formalities are observed in the place of the litigant's domicile, different ones in the place of the contract, different ones in the place where the thing is situated, and different ones in the place of the court. Which formalities are to be regarded? Answer: the formalities, that is, the practice (stylus) of the judge of that forum where the case is litigated, are to be regarded. This is true in general, whether we speak of citizens or of outsiders, for statutes concerning formalities were, in my view, of a mixed kind, and so they exert their force both within and beyond the territory, both with respect to inhabitants and to foreigners. <i>l. 2. in fin.
