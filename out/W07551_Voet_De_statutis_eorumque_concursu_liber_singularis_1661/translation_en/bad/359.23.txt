[1] 3. Does the one who orders a delict also fall under the punishment of the local statute where he gave the order, if the mandatary carries it out, or under the statute of the place where the delict was committed? I answer: although the place of the mandate might seem to be the one to look to, as if he had contracted there with the mandatary, yet since such an unlawful mandate is not a kind of contract, and <i>an action of mandate does not arise between the mandator and the mandatary. Inst. Mandat. §. 7.</i> it seems that the place where the delict was committed or completed must be looked to, and the penalty imposed on the delict by the statute of that place. <i>Arg. l. 16. D. l. Commiss. Clarus, loc. alleg. Abbas in c. licet, n. 27, de foro compet. Decius in l. heres absens, §. apud Labeonem, D. judic. Mascard, d. tract. concl. 7, n. 17, 18; see on Mandate. Baldus in l. 29, §. quid si tantum, D. liber. & posthum.; l. si servum, §. praetor ait, D. Adquir. hered. Bartolus in l. is qui pro emptore, D. Usucap. Salicetus in l. non ideo minus, q. 6, Cod. Accus. Johannes Bertachinus, de Episcop. lib. 2, q. 36, n. 62, p. 75.</i>
