[1] 18. Nor is a rescript valid if it is granted against the law of God, of the apostles, or of the prophets, since it cannot override a superior law. It has been stated elsewhere that an equal cannot command an equal, much less an inferior a superior. Again, it is not valid if it has been obtained against the public law or the public advantage. Hence, if it is harmful to the treasury, one ought not to obey the first command, but should await a second. Unless, however, the right of a third party is not harmed or is harmed only slightly, or the crime is pardoned, or a moratory prescription is granted to a needy debtor, or the Prince expressly derogates from the common law by a clause; on which more carefully in what follows. If it has been obtained for a peremptory prescription and lacks the Prince's subnotation, the date and the consul, it is not valid. Sacred commands, however, perhaps require only the subscription of the Quaestor of the palace.
