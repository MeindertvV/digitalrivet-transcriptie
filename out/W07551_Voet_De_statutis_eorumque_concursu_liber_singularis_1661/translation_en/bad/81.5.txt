[1] 12. A <i>rescript</i> is when the prince responds to the petitions (<i>libelli</i>) of magistrates, judges, or private persons, whether present or absent, who consult him on a point of law. For litigants would offer their petitions to the prince, and he would reply to them on the advice of his council. And if a magistrate had referred to him something more obscure or weighty, he would consult the prince by letter, and the prince would send his answer concerning the case put before him. If the prince replied by rescript to those present, the short reply was called an <i>Adnotatio & Subscriptio</i>. If to those absent, and at greater length, it was called an <i>epistola</i>, or <i>litera</i>. But if the same rescript was issued more carefully and at greater length, not concerning the affairs of private persons but the causes of offices, bodies, orders and colleges, and sometimes too on the report of judges and on the advice of the council, and so that the petitions were not inserted into that rescript (which otherwise held force), it was called a <i>Pragmatica sanctio</i>. <i>l. 19. § 9.
