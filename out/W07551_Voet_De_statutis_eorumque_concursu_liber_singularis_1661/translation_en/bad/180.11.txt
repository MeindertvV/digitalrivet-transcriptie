[1] 6. What if statutes are enacted against the civil law? I answer: if statutes introduce something that the civil law had not introduced, that is, if they determine some case that the civil law had either passed over or left undecided (in which sense something is also said to be contrary to law insofar as it does not arise from that law), then it cannot be denied that those who have the right to enact statutes may lawfully lay down something contrary to, that is, beyond, the civil law. But if statutes expressly enact against the civil law, so far as they partly or wholly derogate from it, then <i>municipalities</i> will not introduce such things unless this has been expressly granted to them. <i>Arg. §. 2. verb. quae sunt juri naturali contraria.
