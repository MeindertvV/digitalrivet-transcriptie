[1] 6. Is the wife bound for her husband's debt, even one contracted before the marriage was entered into, and bound for the whole amount? I answer: although as a general rule one person should not be subjected to an unfair condition because of another, by civil law the wife is in certain cases not liable on her husband's behalf: <i>tit. Cod. ne uxor. pro marito.</i> Yet because the wife is regarded as having tacitly acknowledged her husband's debt by consenting to the statute, <i>the customs of Utrecht, not those of Holland or Friesland, have also approved this very thing, so such a statute is not to be wholly rejected. Arg. last law Cod. de Primipil. Rubric. Stat. Traj. 22. art. 3.</i> This is, however, prevented by antenuptial agreements made before a notary, in which case the community of goods is excluded. <i>Lately by the Ordinance of Utrecht, decision of 1659, April 14, art. 6. Bartol. Chassan. apud Mynsinger. cent. 1. Observat. 29. Gail. 2. observ. 84. num. 11. & 2. observ.</i> 90. in Princ. Treutl. disput. vol. 1, disp. 20, thes. 8, lit. c. Radelant. decis. 21. Christian. Rodenb. tract. de jur. conjug. tit. 4, part. ult., cap. 1, num. 9. Sand. libr. 2, tit. 5, def. 8; see Leeuw. Parat. juris noviss. libr. 4, part. 1, c. 15.
