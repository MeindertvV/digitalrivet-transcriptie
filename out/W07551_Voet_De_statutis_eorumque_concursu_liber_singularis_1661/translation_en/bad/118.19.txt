[1] § As soon as a custom has been introduced and approved, according to the requirements already set out, it produces various and notable effects. In general it has no less force than the law itself, for it differs from law no more than an unwritten thing differs from a written one, and in this respect its power is of the same kind. Hence also custom, where it prevails, is called law and civil law. It admits an interpretation and extension to similar cases equal to that of law, contrary to the view of some who hold that it is of strict law. Hence also whoever transgresses a custom is a despiser of the law. And a judge ordered to judge "according to the laws" is bound to judge according to custom. <i>l. 32. in sin.
