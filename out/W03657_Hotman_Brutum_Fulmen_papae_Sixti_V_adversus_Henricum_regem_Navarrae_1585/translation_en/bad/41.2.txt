[1] Thus far, then, these are the things said about the religions invented under the name of Francis Bernardone. Next let us look at the fictions of the other monk, the old trickster named Dominic. Here the first thing that deserves notice is that odious comparison, by which Dominic (may God, the Father of mercy, forgive us, since we are forced to recount these blasphemies and wicked words) is set against Christ the Lord. For in Antoninus, Bishop of Florence, in his Historiale, part 3, titles 23 and 24, it is written thus: <i>The Lord Christ, he says, is Lord absolutely and by authority. Dominic is Lord by possession. He himself had many divine visions: once he saw Christ wishing to judge and end the world, but the Virgin Mary asked that he wait, and that he send the preachers Dominic and Francis. And again, when he saw heaven opened, and the brothers of every kind of religious order in glory, he did not see his own Dominicans, but it was said to him by the Son of God that they were under the mantle of the Virgin Mary, and there he saw them. And a certain master of the Minorite order, who wrote a large and very instructive book about Blessed Dominic, says that in that vision Dominic could be said to have seen God in his essence, as Paul did in his rapture. Moreover, a great likeness to Christ will appear in him from the course of his life and works. Therefore, because he is like the Lord Christ, Dominic has been most aptly named. Lord, he says: I am the light of the world; the Church sings of Dominic: You are the light of the world. All the prophets bear witness to the Lord. Acts 10. Concerning Dominic and his order, Zechariah, chapter 11, spoke in the person of God: I took to myself two staves, one I called Beauty, the other Cords. Beauty is the order of the Preachers, Cords the order of the Minorites, because they are bound with visible cords.</i> Before Dominic was born into the world, two painted images could be seen at Venice, in the church of St Mark. One of them was of a religious in the habit of the order of the Preachers, with a lily in his hand. The other had the likeness of the Apostle Paul, as he is customarily painted, and over it was written AGIOS PAULOS [Saint Paul]; beneath the figure, at its feet, was written thus: <i>THROUGH THIS MAN ONE GOES TO CHRIST:</i> <i>under the other figure was written</i> AGIOS DOMINIKOS [Saint Dominic]; <i>and under that very figure,</i> <i>MORE EASILY ONE GOES THROUGH THIS MAN.</i> <i>Do not be surprised at writing of this kind, because the teaching of Paul, like that of the other Apostles, was teaching that leads to faith. The teaching of Dominic leads to the observance of the counsels, and therefore one goes more easily through him to Christ. Have you heard these things, you counsellors of the King of France and senators of the Parlements? And will you count among the heretics those who cannot bear these blasphemies?</i> This was once, and is today, as much as ever, the discipline of the Roman Pontiffs and Cardinals, which they put in the place of Holy Scripture in the Christian Church. But let us hear the rest. <i>2. The Lord was born upon the bare earth, but lest he be too much harmed by the cold, his Virgin mother laid him in a manger. Dominic, born an infant, placed under the care of his nurse, when he had got out of his cradle, was often found lying on the bare ground, as if he already shrank from the delights of the flesh.</i> 3. When the Lord was born, a shining star appeared, which led the Magi to him, signifying that through him the whole world would be illuminated. Dominic was born, and at his baptism his spiritual mother saw a star on his forehead, foreshowing a new light of the age. 4. The Lord, at twelve years old, was taken by his parents into the temple and remained there alone as a boy. Dominic, in his boyhood, was taken by his relatives to the church, to be imbued with the divine office, and was left there. 5. From then until youth we find nothing authentic about the Lord, except that he grew in age, as a true man, in wisdom and grace before God and men; but this only apparently. Dominic, however, grew not only in body but also virtually in mind. 6. When the Lord was baptized by John in the Jordan, the Holy Spirit descended upon him in the form of a dove, to signify the fullness of grace, and he went off into the desert. When famine raged in all parts of Spain, and Dominic saw many in want and no one to comfort them, in a fervour of spirit he sold his books and household goods and distributed the price to the poor and gave it away. Then, as if newly and seemingly filled with the Holy Spirit, he at once took up a rule under Bishop Diego, as another baptism and a spiritual desert, separated from delights and worldly things. 7. The Lord gathered a few disciples after himself, and soon multiplied them, sending them two by two to preach the kingdom of God. Dominic, taking a few into religious life with him, was carried on to a copious multitude, whom he appointed to preach the gospel and to heal the sick. 8. The Lord chose poverty for himself and the Apostles, but he had a purse to hold what was offered by the faithful, and Judas carried it; but Dominic was a true lover of poverty, exhorting his brothers to it. 9. The Lord, when he led his disciples, was so hungry that, passing through the corn, they rubbed the ears in their hands to eat them; and when it was already noon and he was weary from the journey, he sent them into Sychar to buy food, and from hunger they sometimes ate without washing their hands. As to the quality of the food, it is generally believed that he ate no meat except the Paschal lamb, out of observance of the law. Dominic the Patriarch, who once passed the whole of Lent, not indeed without food, but on bread and water, tempered this austerity for his followers, granting them wine and delicacies. 10. Perhaps someone will say that the likeness fails here: that the Lord told his disciples, Neither shall you have shoes on your feet, whereas Dominic and his men go out in public shod. But it must be known that that command was temporary, and he dispensed from it at the Last Supper. For we can also see the Lord shod in what John the Baptist says: whose shoe-thongs I am not worthy to loose. Also to Peter in the Acts the Angel said: Put on your sandals; unless someone says he was bound with sandals but not shod, which is not usual. And Mark, his best imitator, brought his broken shoe to the cobbler to be mended. So the Lord and his disciples walked at times shod, at times unshod. And so Dominic, shod in the towns, went out, and on the road often went unshod. 11.</i>
