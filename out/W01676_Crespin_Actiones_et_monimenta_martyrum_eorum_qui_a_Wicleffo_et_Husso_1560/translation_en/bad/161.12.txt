[1] As he left the prison, he commended all the prisoners to God. Most of them wept, especially those who had received some teaching and comfort from him, and they earnestly urged him to be of good and brave heart. When he was led out of the city, he prayed to God for the magistrates and judges by whom he was so treated, and he admonished those who followed him to flee every kind of worship of images. When he came to the bank of the river called the Isara, and the executioner had bound one of his feet, this true servant and witness of Christ asked the sub-prefect to order the executioner to delay a little, until he himself might say a few things. Having obtained this, he began to teach and exhort those who had followed him in great numbers, that with their whole heart and zeal they should follow the Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ, and read it diligently, both for the sake of their salvation set forth in it, and also so that they might avoid those errors, abuses and impieties in which the Pope kept them with great ignorance. "For if you follow him," he said, "you will go headlong into hell. Indeed, how impious their doctrine and discipline are, you can easily recognise from the manner of life they lead in every kind of wickedness and shameful deed. Follow rather Christ the Lord and our Saviour, in whom you will find a truly blessed life, without any danger of death." He said these and many other things for the space of an hour and a half, while those who had followed him listened as if astonished, with great admiration. So great was the grace and wisdom of his speech that most of them, seeing so unworthy a fate befall so great a man, wept most bitterly. But the impious executioner, weary of so long a wait, and who longed more eagerly for a cup of wine than for the saving food of God's Word, ordered him, with great inhumanity, to make an end of speaking. Then Martin, kneeling on the river bank, prayed to God, asking from him forgiveness of all his sins, and calling to mind the death of our Lord Jesus Christ as the ground on which mercy was to be implored and obtained. Finally, this was his last word: "I commend my spirit to you, Lord Jesus." Immediately the executioner fastened a small rope around his neck and twisted it with a short stick until he fell to the ground. Then the executioner pushed him into the river and threw him in, still holding his other foot bound, until he clearly saw that he no longer moved at all. Then he cut the rope and let the body be carried away by the current. And this was the end of Martin, the most true and most steadfast witness of Jesus Christ.
