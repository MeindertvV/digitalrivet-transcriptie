[1] Let us therefore meditate every day on this blessed and excellent death, so that it may be for us a model of the Master, who draws us away from vices and directs us to virtues. Let us hold it in high esteem and take our whole delight in it, since we know it is of the greatest price before the Lord. Let us not spare our foul blood, which is corrupted in us, since God does not esteem it so highly, especially since he seeks it out, remembers it and <i>carefully investigates it, avenging it on the last day, just as the martyrs, or witnesses, of him who did not hesitate to shed their blood for sealing the truth, cry out and demand that it be avenged (Apoc. 6.). But how could our blood not be dear and precious to him, especially since he gathers our tears and stores them in his bottles, so that he will not allow even a single drop to fall or spill away? If our sorrows overwhelm and flood us, he will wipe them away. Are not our sighs and groans, our thoughts and even our most hidden desires all plain and laid open to him? He is the one who searches our hearts through and penetrates every most hidden place. Are not our prayers and cries heard by him too? Therefore the mind must be steeled: let no one find it troublesome or burdensome to heave sighs, to groan, to cry out, to shed tears, to be deprived of his goods, to pour out his blood, to bear and suffer everything even unto death, and that death which is so fearful and strikes such horror into the flesh and into men given over to it. So much so that we who have been born again by the Spirit of the Lord should seek it, love it and embrace it with all joy and cheerfulness of heart, with an unbroken and free spirit, since we see in it so many and great benefits laid up for us, for our neighbours, and above all for our brothers and the Church of God. Indeed, since our blood and ashes are as it were a seed of the godly and of the Church, let us pour out the whole of it, even to the last drop. And yet with calm minds we must await, quietly and with all patience and moderation, a happy outcome, since by this means we possess our souls. This endurance is very necessary for us: through it we have hope, and through it we are tested, so that it may produce proof. Let us therefore do what the Holy Spirit urges us to do through the mouth of David in the last verse of Psal. 27.: "Wait," he says, "for Jehovah, and be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart; wait for Jehovah." Whatever therefore happens, and whatever seems good to God: for that God is our God for ever and ever; he himself will lead us even unto death. May God, the best and greatest, and the Father of mercies, grant us, for the sake of the name of Jesus Christ his Son, that we may lean steadfastly and beupheld by his deeds and promises, with a certain and living faith, by whose power, equipped and armed, we may withstand all our enemies and triumph over them; and so, having conquered Satan and all the gates of hell through Jesus Christ our leader, with whom we can do all things, we shall be victors over all, which is certainly for us a remarkable consolation. Moreover, this life, in which the best and greatest God preserves and protects us, is more burdensome to us because of the wonderful anxiety, distress and sorrow with which we know that you and the whole Church are harassed on our account; and then because of the labours, troubles and expenses which so many godly men undergo and incur for our cause, we who are wretched little men of no use at all. For these reasons, I say, this life is far more bitter to us than any of our own hardships which we endure here. May God always be with you and keep you.</i>
