[1] Ch. 13, v. 9: "And if it bear fruit thenceforth, &c." [Καὶ ἐὰν γένηται καρπὸν εἰς τὸ μέλλον]. Καὶ may be understood as Καὶ ἂν, and why should τὸ ἂν here not be the connecting particle (συμπλοκή), with the sentence complete without an apodosis, aposiopesis or supplement? "Let it alone this year also, till I shall have dig'd about it and cast in dung, and it may bear fruit, but if not, thou shalt afterward cut it downe." Compare that passage in Isocrates: "for in this way you will be persuaded of both, just as if you were to teach them" [ὥστε γὰρ ἀμφοτέρων ὑμεῖς πεπείσεσθε, ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ διδάξαιτε]. The sense is: "for you have proved yourselves in both, so that you could teach them best." See more on ἂν with the subjunctive [ἂν ξυνπαρῶ] in Stephanus, Budaeus and others.
