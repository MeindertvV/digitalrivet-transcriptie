[1] It is not expressed in the Bulls, <i>hic & vbique</i> Terrarum (here and everywhere in the world); but it is only ordered that they shall enjoy the privileges <i>Authoritate Apostolica concessis Doctoribus, Magistris in Studiis generalibus commorantibus</i>, which is to say, according to the custom of the places, <i>feruatis feruandis</i> (those things to be kept being kept). The Bull of Pope election of the University of Louvain gives only the enjoyment of privileges like those granted to the other universities, without expressing them. The Bull of Pope Martin V, given to the Academy of Rostock in the year 1419, says only that those who take the doctorate, having had the licence <i>Authoritate Apostolica ab Episcopo Suerinensi</i>, shall enjoy the privileges, liberties, immunities, and grants made to doctors and masters who lecture, in conformity with the Academies of Cologne, Vienna, Leipzig, and Padua. This Bull was confirmed by Pope Innocent, and by Ferdinand I, after this Academy had become Lutheran, which Emperor gives power <i>in omnibus locis & Terris sacri Romani Imperij</i> (in all places and lands of the Holy Roman Empire); and <i>vbique Terrarum</i> (everywhere in the world), (note these new words:) <i>habere omnes actus Doctorum, legendi, interpretandi, docendi, glossandi, facere, & exercere possint</i> (that they may be able to have and perform all the acts of doctors: reading, interpreting, teaching, glossing, making, and practising). Behold the new concessions which the <i>Lutheran Academies</i> have had inserted into the Bulls of Imperial foundation. We see them all alike in the letters of foundation given to the University of <i>Strasbourg</i> by the Emperor <i>Maximilian II</i>, confirmed and enlarged by the Emperor <i>Ferdinand II</i>. In these <i>Lutheran Academies</i>, which took their foundation from the <i>Emperor</i>, they have a Chancellor, who is a lord of rank of the country or of the city, who gives in writing, <i>Authoritate Caesarea aut Imperatoria</i> (by Imperial or Caesarean authority), the permission to the Promoter, who is the doctor who must preside over the act and give the cap, to confer the dignity of doctor on one, or two, or three together. But before the Promoter pronounces the words of the ceremony, the candidate must kneel to receive this grace, with more submission on the part of the <i>Emperor</i>. For medicine, these words are pronounced in this way: <i>Ego pro ea Facultate, quæ mihi ab Amplissimo Nobilissimoque D. Cancellario data est, & publicè in hac illustri, splendidaque corona, supremo gradu</i> in arte <i>Medica dono, & in Medicinæ Doctorem promoveo, in nomine sacrosanctæ & individuæ Trinitatis, Patris, & Filij, & Spiritus Sancti. Deinde omnia ornamenta & Privilegia, quæ Medicinæ Doctoribus à summis concessa sunt principibus attribuo.</i> (I, for that Faculty which has been given to me by the Most Illustrious and Most Noble Lord Chancellor, and publicly in this illustrious and splendid assembly, bestow the highest degree in the art of medicine, and promote you to the doctorate of medicine, in the sacred name of the most holy and undivided Trinity, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Then I assign to you all the ornaments and privileges which have been granted by the highest princes to doctors of medicine.)
