[1] So I seek the common angle of the two intersecting lines, under this sign ⊙, where you will find written the diet from the foods that are eaten that will suit this condition. You shall give this diet for as long as both the strength and the disease remain in this state. But suppose that after three days the aforesaid young man's symptoms and fever have increased, so that on the day of intermission they do not disappear but are somewhat remitted, while his strength is as before. Seek the disease under c, and the common place under the sign Δ, where you will find thick broths, which you will give to the young man. But the plan of diet is not only set according to the whole disease, but also according to the particular paroxysm. For for the most part, from the beginning of the paroxysms up to the onset of the decline, the sufferers are tormented and restless, and do not keep to one position in bed. When the decline begins, however, they remain in one place and bear the illness more tolerably. Since these things are so, the diseases press hardest from the beginnings of the attacks up to the state; but the strength of the sufferers for the most part is not pressed during that time, for strength cannot be wasted away in a short interval of time. Therefore, since during that whole period from the beginning to the state the disease is under a, and the strength of the sufferer under f, the common place will be under the sign Ѱ, where you will find written complete abstinence in every respect. Hippocrates, the greatest of the rational physicians, advised this to be used in the attacks, reasonably, from the beginnings of the paroxysms up to their decline. But as far as the particular attacks are concerned, it must be understood that strength is not pressed unless it is pressed most of all. For even if it is pressed greatly, yet at that time, which ends at the beginning of the paroxysm's decline, strength is not pressed unless it is pressed most of all. Hence it comes about that when the patient is near a faint, or is already seized by a swooning of the spirits, food must be given to him so that his strength may be preserved, even if it is the hour of the following paroxysm.
