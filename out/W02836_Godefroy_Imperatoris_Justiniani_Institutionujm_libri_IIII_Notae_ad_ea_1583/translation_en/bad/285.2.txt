[1] Suetonius, in Augustus's Letter, chapter 76: "I ate an ounce of bread with a few grapes of the hard-berried vine." So the duracina vine, like the dried fig (ficus duricoria). Duracina is named from the hardness of its berries (duritia acinorum). Pliny, book 14: it lasts against the cold because of its firmness. The kinds of grapes are: Aminea, asinusca, atrusca, albuierus, albena, apiana, apicia, bumniammia, aur, as the Greeks call it [βύμμασος], duracina, labrusca, melampsithia, maroma, mareotis, numentana, precia, prannia, psithia, pilliolata, rhodia, stephanitis, venunucula, variola, lagea. Macrobius, Saturnalia, book 3, last chapter.
