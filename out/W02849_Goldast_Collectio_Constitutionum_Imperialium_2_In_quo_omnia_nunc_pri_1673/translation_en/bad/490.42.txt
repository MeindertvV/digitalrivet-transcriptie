[1] <i><i>Likewise, since provision must be made for the universal Church of God, which against unbelievers, heretics, schismatics and corrupters of good morals especially needs learned men who are skilled in knowledge, and who spend their income and their own patrimony on their studies and other labours, and since the ordinaries would perhaps provide for such men either not at all or only later, this holy council decrees and ordains that a fourth part of all ecclesiastical benefices be reserved for learned men. These are the benefices whose value is judged to exceed ten marks of silver. Specifically, in every cathedral church a fourth part of the canonries and prebends is to be reserved for Doctors of Theology, of Canon Law and of Civil Law, or for licentiates in those faculties. In each collegiate church likewise, a fourth part of the canonries and prebends that carry larger stipends and exceed the said value is to be reserved for these doctors and licentiates, or for doctors, masters and bachelors in Medicine. The same applies to a fourth part of the benefices in the gift of any other ordinary patron. Thus, for the nomination to that fourth part, the hands of all ordinaries are to be closed after such greater benefices fall vacant, as the decrees of the supreme pontiffs have hitherto laid down. By this rule the ordinary patrons are to wait, and the aforesaid doctors, licentiates, masters and bachelors are to come forward and urge that they be admitted. But if such men do not come within the time allowed, or do not press for admission; or if they press but do not wish to serve the benefices; or if, though willing to serve, they are perhaps unfit and unsuitable for them: then the ordinary patrons may confer the vacant benefices, on this occasion only, upon other suitable persons, even if they lack degrees. Yet this must not prejudice the aforesaid doctors, licentiates, masters and bachelors in regard to future vacancies in the said fourth part. Any collations made otherwise or in another way shall be void by law. Moreover, patrons who confer ecclesiastical benefices contrary to this, or in opposition to it, shall by that very act incur suspension from conferring other benefices, if they have any further to confer, and from the revenues of their own benefices on account of which such collation is due to them, for three years. Besides this, both the patron and the person to whom the benefice was improperly provided are to be corrected and reformed in the province, in a provincial or episcopal synod, according to its judgement, so that the rest may take warning from the example.</i></i>
