[1] <i><i>On the appointed day of the imperial coronation, which is most suitably the Sunday Laetare in the middle of Lent (as Durantes wrote in his Rationale of the Divine Offices, and as was observed by Nicholas V at the coronation of Frederick III), the church of St Peter and the greater chapel shall be decently adorned, as they are customarily decorated when the Pontiff is about to celebrate the divine offices on the greatest solemnities. Besides the usual things, two platforms are set up on either side outside the chancel of the chapel: one for the elected Emperor, on the left as those approaching enter, and the other on the right for the Empress. The whole church is hung with tapestries, hangings, seats, footstools and other ornaments, and above all the chapel of St George, the chapel of St Maurice, and the chapel called Blessed Mary between the towers, which lies outside the church in the first porch beneath the bell tower, are thoroughly cleaned and decorated. The Empress, before the Pontiff comes down to the church, arrives with her attendants and mounts the platform prepared for her there. Early in the morning the Pontiff, with the Cardinals, prelates and other officials, vested in colours suited to the season and the Mass, as for a procession, comes down to the church under a canopy. He sends ahead two Cardinal deacons in their woollen copes to the Emperor, to escort him to the church at the proper time. The Pontiff, after making reverence before the altar, goes to the seat where he is accustomed to receive his vestments, and there receives the reverence of all the prelates who are vested. He receives the third, and takes the sandals as usual, and after the prayer is said he is clothed in all the pontifical vestments. Meanwhile the Cardinals who are with the Emperor, as soon as they have seen the Pontiff enter the church, come down to the church with the Emperor and all his retinue. Having gone out through the first door of the palace and through the first doors of the church, they come to the chapel called Blessed Mary between the towers. There they are received by the canons and the college of the church, who come to meet them. Once the Emperor has been led there, at the altar of the said chapel, into the hands of the first Cardinal present, who receives in the name of the Pontiff, he swears the customary oath to Blessed Peter, to the supreme Pontiff and to his successors, in the form written below, namely: <i>I, N., King of the Romans, with the Lord's help to be Emperor, promise, pledge and vow, and swear to God and Blessed Peter, that I will be the outside protector and defender of the supreme Pontiff and of the holy Roman Church in all its needs and advantages, guarding and preserving its possessions, honours, rights and law, as far as I shall be able and know how, aided by the divine help, with a right and pure faith. So may God help me, and these holy Gospels of God.</i> Then he is vested in a surplice and almuce, and is received by the canons of St Peter as a canon and a brother. All of them stand at the altar of the said chapel, and he receives them to the kiss of peace. Then, with the clergy going before and the response being sung, <i>Peter, do you love me? You know, Lord, that I love you. Feed my sheep.</i> Verse: <i>Simon, son of John, do you love me more than these? You know, Lord, that I love you. Feed, etc.</i> The other nobles of the Emperor come in their order to the principal door of the basilica, which is called the Silver Door and is the middle of the five. Meanwhile the Cardinal bishops, after the senior ones, make reverence to the Pontiff, and with their mitres and copes come to the aforesaid door while the Emperor is still in the said chapel between the towers. Before him the junior Cardinal bishop, standing without a mitre over the Emperor kneeling in prayer, says this prayer: <i>Let us pray. God, in whose hand are the hearts of kings, incline the ears of your mercy to our humble prayers, and grant to this your servant the King true wisdom, so that he may rule well, drawing counsel from your fountain, and may please you and excel above all, through Christ our Lord. Amen.</i> When the first prayer is finished they enter the church, the Emperor always walking between the two Cardinal deacons mentioned, and immediately preceded by the two Cardinal bishops mentioned; turning to the left, they come to the chapel of St George. There the Emperor, sitting, lays aside the almuce and first puts on the sandals, then, standing, the tunicle, and at last the imperial mantle. Then, going forward at once, he comes to the middle of the church, where the porphyry disc is, and there another Cardinal bishop standing beside him says another prayer over him, namely: <i>O God, inexpressible author of the world, creator of the human race, governor of the empire, confirmer of kingdoms, who from the womb of Abraham, our patriarch, your faithful friend, chose a king to rule over the ages, surround this present King with his army, through the intercession of the saints, with the abundant blessing of eternity, so that they may always remain joyful and triumphant, victors in peace, through Christ our Lord. Amen.</i> Then they proceed to the confession of Blessed Peter beneath the altar, where the Emperor throws himself down upon the faldstool. The two Cardinal bishops go up into the chapel and go to their seats. The senior of the deacons and the senior of the presbyters, vested, come down to him and kneel on either side, and the deacon himself sings the Litany, lying prostrate on the left, while all reply in a loud voice and remain kneeling. When the Litany is finished, the senior of the presbyters rises and, over the Emperor who is still prostrate, says: <i>Our Father, and lead us not into temptation. Save your servant, O my God, who trusts in you.</i> Verse: <i>Let the enemy gain nothing against him, and let the son of iniquity not be able to harm him.</i> Verse: <i>Lord, hear my prayer, and let my cry come to you.</i> Verse: <i>The Lord be with you, and with your spirit.</i> Let us pray. <i>We beseech you, Lord, stretch out to your servant the right hand of heavenly help, so that he may seek you with his whole heart, and obtain what he rightly asks. We beg you, Lord, to anticipate our actions by inspiring them and to follow them by helping them, so that all our prayer and work may always begin from you and be completed through you. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.</i> When these are finished with the deacon, he returns to his place among the other Cardinals. While the final prayers are said by another Cardinal presbyter, the senior of the Cardinal bishops, with the pluvial, stole and mitre, comes down to the Emperor, and together with the two deacons, when the prayers are finished, leads him to the altar of St Maurice. There, standing with the mitre, he anoints the Emperor's right arm and shoulder-blades in the form of a cross with exorcised oil, and, laying aside the mitre, says: <i>Let us pray. Almighty God, to whom all power and dignity belong, we beseech you with suppliant prayer and most humble entreaty, that you grant to this your servant N. the successful completion of the imperial majesty, so that, established under your protection to rule your holy Church, nothing present may stand in his way and nothing future may oppose him; but, with the gift of the Holy Spirit breathed into him, may he be able to rule the people subject to him with the even balance of justice, and in all his works may always fear you and strive continually to please you. May God's Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who was anointed by God the Father above his fellows, pour through the present infusion of the holy ointment the blessing of the Spirit the Paraclete upon his head, and make the same penetrate even to the inmost of your heart, so that by this visible and tangible gift he may deserve to perceive invisible things and, by just moderation, to reign eternally with him in the temporal kingdom, who without sin lives as King of kings with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen.</i> Meanwhile the Empress, if she is present, is brought in and anointed in the same places in the same way. Then the same Cardinal bishop, with his mitre, says a prayer over her, since the prayer is not addressed to her but to God: <i>May the grace of the Holy Spirit descend upon you abundantly through the office of our humility, so that, just as your outward appearance is made fat by the material oil smeared on by our unworthy hands, so too she may deserve to be made inwardly rich through its visible anointing, and may learn and be able, imbued with the most perfect spiritual anointing, to turn away from and despise unlawful things with her whole mind, and may continually desire and do the things that are useful to your soul, with the help of our Lord Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns with the Father and the Holy Spirit, God, etc.</i> These things are done at the altar of Blessed Maurice; for at the altar of Blessed Peter no one is anointed or ordained except the Roman Pontiff alone. When these things are completed, the Empress returns to her gallery. The Emperor, with the Cardinal bishop and the deacons, goes to the Pontiff; the deacons, having received their dalmatics and mitres in their hands, go to make reverence to the Pope. He, coming down from his seat, goes to the altar in supplication, as usual, and receives to the kiss of the mouth and the foot three Cardinal presbyters who are assisting, while he remains kneeling on his faldstool. When the confession is finished the Pontiff goes to the altar, kisses it, and incenses it, as on other occasions. Then he receives to the kiss of the mouth and the foot first the Emperor, then his three Cardinal deacons. And at once he ascends to the high seat, and the Emperor is led to the highest platform with his principal nobles and officers. When the Pontiff has withdrawn from the side, the Emperor's officers bring the sword with its scabbard and belt, the crown, the sceptre and the orb, which are to be given to the Emperor and Empress at the proper time; these are placed upon the altar by the sacristan and the master of ceremonies. Meanwhile, while the Pontiff makes the confession, the choir begins the Introit and the Kyrie. The Pontiff, standing without a mitre on his high seat, reads the Introit and proceeds in the Mass as on other occasions as far as the said Epistle and gradual. But after the collect of the day he says this prayer for the Emperor himself: <i>Let us pray. God, protector of all kingdoms and especially of the Christian Empire, grant to your servant N., our Emperor, to recall with understanding the triumph of your virtue, so that he who is made prince by your appointment may always be made powerful by your gift.</i> Over the Secret: <i>Receive, Lord, the offerings of your Church for the salvation of your servant N., who asks for it, and for the protection of the faithful people. Raise up the wonders of your arm, so that, with enemies overcome, he may serve you in secure Christian liberty, through our Lord Jesus Christ your only-begotten Son, etc.</i> After the Communion: <i>O God, who prepared the Roman Empire to preach the Gospel of the eternal kingdom, stretch out to your servant, our Emperor N., heavenly arms, so that no peace may be disturbed by the temporal troubles of war, through our Lord Jesus Christ.</i> While the gradual is sung, the Emperor comes down from the platform to the feet of our most holy Lord, led by the two Cardinal deacons who are vested; and one of the clerics of the chamber takes the sword with its scabbard from the altar and carries it on the right to the senior of the deacons, who stands before the Pontiff. The Pontiff, taking it from the hand of that deacon, sitting, with the mitre, hands it over to the Emperor kneeling before him, in his right hand, saying: <i>Receive the sword taken from above the body of the Apostle Blessed Peter by our unworthy hands, yet consecrated by the power and authority of Almighty God and of the blessed Apostles, granted for the imperial office, and by the office of our blessing, divinely given for the defence of holy Mother Church, for the punishment of evildoers and for the praise of the good.</i> And remember of whom the Psalmist prophesied, saying: <i>Gird your sword upon your thigh, most mighty one, so that in this you may exercise the force of equity, and by the same you may destroy the mass of iniquity, most mighty one, and may defend and protect the holy Church and its faithful, and no less exterminate and scatter the enemies of the faith than the enemies of the Christian name, may kindly help and defend widows and orphans, restore what is desolate, preserve what is restored, and protect the wretched from injury, preserving those well ordered; so that by doing this you may deserve, as a glorious conqueror of virtues and an outstanding worshipper of justice, to reign without end with the Saviour of the world (whose type you bear) in his name. He who lives with the Father and the Spirit, etc.</i> When the prayer is finished, the deacon takes the sword and returns it to the scabbard which he held in his hand, and together with the Pontiff girds the Emperor with it, the Pontiff saying: <i>Gird your sword upon your thigh, most mighty one. And take note that the saints conquered kingdoms not by the sword, but by faith.</i> Girded with the sword, the Emperor, made a soldier of Blessed Peter, rises, draws the sword, brandishes it three times, then returns it to the scabbard and kneels again. To him the Pontiff, having taken the orb from the altar and the sceptre (as I said of the sword) carried in the same way, places the orb in his right hand and the sceptre in his left, saying: <i>Receive the rod of virtue and of righteousness, by which you may understand that you are bound to soothe the pious, to terrify the reprobate, to teach the erring the way, to stretch out a hand to the fallen, to cast down the proud and to raise up the humble.</i> And may Jesus Christ our Lord open the door to you, who opens by himself and no one shuts, shuts and no one opens. And may he be your guide, who led the prisoner out of the house of confinement, sitting in darkness and the shadow of death, so that you may deserve to follow him in all things, of whom the prophet David sang: <i>Your sceptre, Jesus, is for ever and ever, the rod of direction, the rod of your kingdom.</i> And in imitating him, love justice and hate iniquity; for that reason God, your God, anointed you, as an example of him whom he had anointed before the ages with the oil of gladness above his fellows, Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with him as God, etc. Last, he places the imperial crown, brought to him from the altar with its tiara (as we have said of the others), upon his head, saying: <i>Receive the sign of glory and the diadem of the kingdom, the crown of the Empire, in the name of the † Father, and of the † Son, and of the † Holy Spirit; so that, having rejected the ancient enemy and having rejected the contagions of all vices, you may love justice, mercy and judgment in this way, and may live justly, piously and mercifully, so that from our Lord Jesus Christ himself you may receive the crown of the eternal kingdom in the company of the saints, who with the Father, etc.</i> At once the Emperor kisses the feet of the Pontiff, and rising, he is unbuckled from the sword, which he hands to his armour-bearer. Then he himself, with the crown, sceptre and orb, goes forward and is led by the two deacons to the imperial seat prepared for him on the right of the Pontiff, between the Pontiff and the senior of the Cardinal bishops. This seat is lower than the seats of the Cardinals and lower than the papal seat; it has a golden cloth behind and a small footstool. And he will sit there until the Empress is crowned. The Empress is led by the same men to the Pontiff, who rises without a mitre and, over her kneeling, says this prayer: <i>O God, who alone have immortality, and dwell in unapproachable light, whose wisdom is not deceived in its arrangement, who make all things that are to come and call those things that are not as though they were, who by your higher government cast down from rule and graciously raise the humble to high places: we humbly beseech your unspeakable mercy, that just as you caused Esther, queen of Israel, for the cause of salvation, to be freed from the shackles of her captivity and to pass to the king's bed and the partnership of his kingdom, so you may grant that this your servant N., by the blessing of our humility, for the saving grace of the Christian people, may pass into the worthy and exalted union of our Emperor and into the participation of his Empire; and that, sitting in the imperial seat, always remaining chaste, she may be able to hold the palm nearest to virginity, and may continually desire to please you, the true and living God, in all things and above all things, and that, with your inspiration, she may perform with her whole heart what is pleasing to you, through Christ our Lord. Amen.</i> Then, sitting with the mitre, he takes the imperial crown from the hand of the senior of the Cardinal deacons, brought from the altar, and places it upon her head, saying: <i>By the office of our unworthiness, N., Empress, solemnly blessed, receive the crown of imperial excellence, which is placed upon your head not by an unworthy hand, but by episcopal hands, so that, just as you shine adorned with gold and gems, so may almighty God grant that you be adorned with the mitres of gold of wisdom and virtue, with gems, so that after the end of this age, meeting worthily and praiseworthily with the wise virgins our eternal Bridegroom, our Lord Jesus Christ, you may deserve to enter the gate of the heavenly court, with Christ helping, who lives with the Father, etc.</i> Crowned, she at once kisses the feet of the Pontiff, and rising is led, she and the Emperor together, to their platforms, where they are placed in the seats prepared for them. When the Emperor and the Augusta are seated in their chairs with the imperial ornaments, the senior of the apostolic subdeacons, with the other subdeacons and the chaplains of the imperial court, goes down to the confession of Blessed Peter and, with them, sings praises to the Emperor in a loud voice, saying: <i>Hear us, Christ.</i> The secretaries of the Ubii, standing in silk copes above, near the choir, respond: <i>To Lord Frederick, most invincible Roman Emperor and ever Augustus, health and victory.</i> When this praise has been repeated three times, the senior of the deacons with his three companions says: <i>Saviour of the world.</i> The secretaries in turn say: <i>Help him.</i> Help him. And then he, with his two companions, says: Holy Mary. And they in turn respond: Help him too. And so on in order. Afterwards, when all have returned to their places, the Alleluia or the tract is sung, and then the Gospel, the Creed and the Offertory. Then the Emperor, with the crown and mantle laid aside, without the sceptre or orb, comes down and offers gold at the feet of the Pontiff, as much as he pleases. The Empress likewise does the same afterwards; she then does not return to the platform, but kneels upon the faldstool prepared for her on the right side of the altar, in a place where she does not hinder the credence table, and there she waits until the sermon. The Emperor follows the Pontiff as he goes down to the altar, and offers him the chalice and paten with the wafers in place of the subdeacon, and then the water to be poured into the wine. Withdrawing to the right side, he stands until the Pontiff returns to his high seat to communicate; the Emperor follows him, and from his hands he himself first, then the Augusta, receive the holy Communion of the Body of Christ, after the Pontiff has communicated with the deacon and the subdeacon. For the subdeacon, after he has communicated with the deacon of the Body and Blood of Christ, receives from the deacon's hand at the altar, with the customary circles and ceremonies, two consecrated hosts for the Emperor and the Augusta, and carries them to the Pontiff. And when the Sacrament has been received, the Emperor indeed kisses the face of the Pontiff, but the Augusta kisses his hand; and both, descending to the two platforms, there resume the imperial insignia. Yet the Pontiff may, if it seems good to him, immediately after he has communicated the Emperor kneeling before him with the Augusta, say the prayers written at the beginning, namely: Look upon us, we beseech you, Almighty God, ...
