<i><i>And a little later: On this point it must be noted that the present book takes its name threefold, in three ways. First, it is said to be the Wichbild, that is, Municipal Law, a name which ... from an ancient allotment it has held since the time ... when the son of Ham had built the city of Babylon, ... to all broadly, it is law, which is derived from Wico or from fortifications, and interprets Wichbild or Municipal Law, as if law ... of this kind extends as far as ... that law is wider ... it has hitherto, and remains unshaken. That law also has its origin in natural law, which remains ... that law, Frenrecht, that is, Holy Law, or the law of God. For just as God is incomprehensible and immense ... yet he can be understood under this, that he is the communicator of all good things; so by a similar reasoning ... no one in it ought to seek his own advantage, but that law ought to be common to all. Thirdly, it is called law ... recht, that is, Civil Law, because it is given to the cities themselves, and the cities around the Empire ought to stand firmly and unshaken ... according to their strength. Hence, as the text has it, the Emperor entrusts the outlying cities to the waters ... fortresses, bulwarks and other fortifications.</i></i>
