[1] ## PART II. Chapter 1. It is argued that the Pope may receive money for ecclesiastical benefices. Several laws and sayings of the doctors are cited for this, and those sayings are condemned; and an answer is given to the laws. 1541

[2] 2. In which the opinion of the aforesaid doctors is rejected by the authority of sacred theology and Scripture, and it is proved that the Pope cannot receive money for benefices, and also that, so far as the Pope is concerned, it is real simony. 1542

[3] 3. In which the same point is proved, as above, from canon law and by the authority of a general council. 1543

[4] 4. In which the same point is established by arguments, which are multiplied in the two preceding chapters. 1544

[5] 5. In which, first, the aforesaid distinction concerning simoniacal acts is overturned, on the ground that they are forbidden by canon law. Secondly, it is declared that nothing is simoniacal by its nature with respect to their intention. Likewise, that nothing is simoniacal merely because it is forbidden. 1548
