[1] [The remainder of the body text continues in the same manner down the page; the full transcription of the long letter follows the same pattern.] The supreme Pontiff seeks, as if now openly to make it plain, that nothing is closer to his heart than that Julius Caesar once brought home a triumph over conquered enemies: so he himself may one day triumph over the enemies and the wrecked Church of God, that is, over its good members (God forbid). Therefore the holy Synod, in the decrees of its first session, revoked and annulled, in the manner of the Council of Constance, everything done or to be done in any way under that most holy indiction, against the indicters or other supposed councils; and it asks and urges that your Majesty assist and carry this out. Therefore lift up your ears, Caesar. He who expressly calls the cardinals who issued the indiction schismatics and heretics openly proclaims your Majesty and the most Christian kings, with one and the same voice, to be schismatics and heretics. For the indiction of this council was made by your assent and your help. Hence it seems strange that the Most Holy Father did not notice that the import of such a sentence is hinted at and even bandied about among those whom he ought to have understood to be condemned by that same sentence no less than the cardinals themselves. Rise up, then, best of Caesars; be present, be watchful. The Church totters, the good are oppressed, the impious are raised up, justice sinks, impiety is cultivated, and the unfaithful rise up and are received into favour. But those who take counsel for the Church, and bring help and aid to it, are called enemies, are oppressed and overwhelmed. Come, greatest Caesar: the Church itself, whose advocate and protector Almighty God has made you, namely the Roman and universal Church, calls upon your Majesty. With a loud and piteous voice she asks for your aid. She supplicates, entreats and beseeches your Highness, with both palms joined, not to allow her to be driven headlong. The holy Synod of Pisa, which represents her, also prays this and asks it with all the prayers it can muster. That this may be granted more promptly and eagerly, we trust that your Majesty will do it of your own accord, through your innate piety and religion, through the greatness of your lofty spirit, and also through the dignity of the illustrious lesser princes. We wish you to remember one thing: since this holy Synod was indicted and assembled with your assent and aid, no injury can come to it without offence to your Highness. Therefore, with the same spirit and force with which you are accustomed, by the authority of the Empire, to restrain those who offend your Majesty, it is most just that you pursue the violators and disturbers of sacred things and councils, especially out of regard for God and religion, of which you are most zealous, at the instance of the Synod of Pisa. We urge and beseech you again and again to do this as soon as possible and from your heart. A response lately published, which fully and abundantly sets forth, with the greatest gravity and wisdom, both the justice of our indiction and assembly and how slight and fragile are the points raised by the consultors of the supreme Pontiff against the letter of the indicted and so-called Council of the Lateran, has been adapted by the venerable Cardinals and the other indicters of this holy council to those same letters. It is entitled the Apology of the Council of Pisa and begins "Our Creator and Redeemer". Therefore, lest we exceed the limits of a letter, we will not repeat it here. May your Imperial Majesty fare well and prosper.
