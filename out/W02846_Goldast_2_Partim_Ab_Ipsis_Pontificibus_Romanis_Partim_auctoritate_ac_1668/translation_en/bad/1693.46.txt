[1] XIII. Finally, as to the Council that was called by the supreme Pontiff at Rome and was held to be ordinary, we say that since the reformation of the head is to be dealt with, the convening of the Council is not within the proper competence of the supreme Pontiff. Even if it were, his manifest negligence excludes him, so that the delay cannot be excused, as was said above. And even if a delay could be excused, a Council in the city of Rome is not safe in these times, as is well known. For who in Rome would dare to call the supreme Pontiff to account for his stewardship? He is accustomed to rage against anyone who opposes his will, as noted by Bald. <i>in l. de pupillo §. si quis ipsi Prætori, de operis nouis nunciat</i>. And the non-appearance of one summoned to a place that is not safe is a most just excuse: <i>c. expe-te de app. l. Clem. Pastoralis §. Pisana de re iudic. c. accedens el. 2. ut lite non contest.</i> And since the supreme Pontiff pursues the most reverend Cardinals with deadly hatred, they ought not to trust letters of safe conduct under the said chapter: <i>accedens, & in cap. literas in fine, de restit. spoliat.</i> And although the most reverend Lords Cardinals could defend themselves on these most excellent and most evident grounds, and could persist further in the Council previously called at Pisa, nevertheless, considering that their good intention and sincerity of mind should be made known to all by their deeds, since they seek nothing except that the welfare of the universal Church be provided for, and that every occasion of the impending schism be removed, they have offered, and do offer, that they are most ready to obey the supreme Pontiff and to show him due reverence, provided that his Holiness is willing, by common agreement, to appoint a safe place for a general Council. And that he may be pleased to do this entirely, they have asked, and again ask and beseech his Holiness with all reverence and humility. For in this the whole machinery of the present dispute is resolved. For if the supreme Pontiff is unwilling to admit this most just and equitable offer, and, as he has hitherto done, has reserved and rejected it, the manifest truth will appear (which cannot be evaded) that he himself does not wish a free Council to be convened at all. But, <i>non solum ff. de action. & obligat., non tantum enim verbis, sed etiam facto voluntas declaratur, l. Paulus ff. rem rat. hab. eam dilecti[?], el l. de app. facit text. 22. quæst. 5. in Can. Canete 11. quæst. 3. in can. exstirpanda</i>: and in such a case the Fathers of the Council of Pisa, as was said above, can and ought to proceed further by the best right. Nor does it matter that the supreme Pontiff has forbidden such a Council under the most grievous censures, because in this he is not to be obeyed, and they ought not to fear his censure. This is argued at length in the very terms by Antonius Rosellus in his treatise <i>de potestate Papæ, in charta 55. in sin.</i>: since such a command against a Council is unlawful, it is not to be obeyed, the chapter <i>literas, de restit. spoliat.</i> being cited. To this the text in can. non semper 11. quæst. 3 applies, where it is said that a prelate is not always to be obeyed: hence, if he commands something against God, or forbids what God has commanded, he is not to be obeyed, 11. quæst. 3, in can. si is qui præst, <i>Nam Deo magis quam hominibus obediendum est</i>, 8. distinct. can. quæ contra, in sin., and 11. quæst. 3, in can. si Dominus. In such a case a sentence of excommunication or interdict does not burden one before God, nor before his Church: said can. si is qui præst, and in can. nemo debet, 11. quæst. 3. The Abbot teaches the same in the very terms in cap. inquisitioni, near the end, de sentent. excommunicat., where he says that the Cardinals who withdrew from Gregory XII, because he would not observe his vow and oath, although they had been recalled under the most grievous censures, were not bound to obey, since the Council had been impeded. Peter of Ancarano also held the same view, <i>in consil. 92. pro declaratione dubiorum, col. sin.</i>, where he says in the very terms that the most reverend Lords Cardinals are bound to pursue the Council to which they bound themselves by vow and oath, notwithstanding the prohibition of the Pope. And so it seems that the law should be concluded. These matters have been stated briefly, as I think, for the sake of truth and justice, and for the confirmation of the Counsel published by me; all of which turn upon matters of law. Therefore, since they have been written with the good favour of our most holy Lord, and subject to the correction of the Apostolic See, I shall say nothing about those matters which consist in fact and concern the person of our most holy Lord, because I know nothing, and it is not for me to set my mouth against heaven (can. in tantum, 21. distinct.); and for the holiness and integrity of life of him whom the summit of so great a dignity raises up, one must presume well (can. non nos, 40. distinct.). Praise be to God.
