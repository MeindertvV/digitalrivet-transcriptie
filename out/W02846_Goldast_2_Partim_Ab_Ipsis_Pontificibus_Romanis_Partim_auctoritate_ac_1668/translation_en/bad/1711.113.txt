[9] 66. Here begins the second principal part of this sixth book, in which the author intends to investigate the punishment of a heretical Pope. And first, the penalty of his deposition from the papacy and from every ecclesiastical dignity, and how a Pope, or any other, if he truly becomes a heretic and is not merely falsely accused, is to be struck down and punished canonically. To determine this, first it is asked whether a Pope, if he truly becomes a heretic, is ipso facto deprived of all authority and ecclesiastical dignity. The answer is yes. First, it is shown that from divine law can be deduced the first assertion or conclusion of the aforesaid question, namely that a Pope, if he truly becomes a heretic, by that very fact is no true Pope, and by divine law and the sentence of Christ is by that very fact deprived of the papacy. This is shown by fifteen arguments.
