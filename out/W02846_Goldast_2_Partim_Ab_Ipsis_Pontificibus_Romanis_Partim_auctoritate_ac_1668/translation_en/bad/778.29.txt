[1] Likewise, for this frequently repeated error they argue as follows: the Apostle says in 1 Cor. 18 that <i>all indeed run, but one receives the prize</i>, where the Gloss says that <i>although only one receives the prize here, yet in the other life all receive it together; and so before the day of judgment no holy soul will have the prize of the vision of the divine essence.</i> But these men, so that they may confirm their errors by authentic Scriptures, do not care to attend to other passages of Scripture which plainly declare the true sense of the Scriptures and which seem to speak against them. For if they had read the Gloss on the words of the Apostle in Heb. 11, (<i>and these all, having obtained a good report through faith, did not receive the promise, God having provided some better thing for us, that they without us should not be made perfect</i>) and had understood the reasoning, they would never have cited it in support of their error about the words of the Apostle, <i>one receives the prize</i>. Yet the Gloss, which these men pervert and mutilate, says in the same place: <i>this example shows how great is the advantage of our law, in which the palm is promised not to one but to all; this is not so in that state of this age and in that expectation, for there only one receives the prize and the others depart defeated, although they have laboured in the same way; but here it is not so, for all who run, if they run perseveringly, receive the prize, and he who comes first should wait, not to be crowned with that prize which is the robe of the soul together with the later one, but he should wait</i> (according to the Gloss) <i>to be crowned with that prize which is the robe of the body together with the later one.</i> The Gloss also clearly asserts this on the words of the Apostle in Heb. 11, <i>these all, having obtained a good report through faith</i>, saying: <i>and these ancient faithful, through faith, did and suffered such great things for the faith, and yet these all, having obtained a good report through faith</i> (that is, what faith has testified of them) <i>have not yet received the promise: that is, the full blessedness of body and soul, that is, the two robes; that is, if God, providing some better thing for us, had not made them each to receive one, so that they should not be made perfect and fulfilled without us, so that in the common joy of all the joy of each might be greater; for if they have received each one robe, they will not yet have a double robe until the common resurrection, so that the completion of all the faithful may happen together.</i> By these words it is plainly shown how the Gloss must be understood when it says: <i>he who comes first should wait to be crowned with the later one</i>, because he waits as regards the one robe of the body, and does not wait as regards the other robe, namely the robe of the soul. Therefore the Gloss, agreeing expressly with blessed Gregory in the fourth book of the Dialogues, as was cited above, is to be preferred to these fantastic verse-writings, which desire to please the heretical man; and since God will confound them, the truth is to be embraced more dearly and defended more manfully.
