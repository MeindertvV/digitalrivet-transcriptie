[1] ## Book IV. Where it is asked whether Christ appointed Peter the principal of the other Apostles or not, and the different ways of speaking are set out.
[2] Chapter 1. The first opinion, namely that Christ
[3] did not appoint Peter as prince
[4] and prelate of the other Apostles and of all
[5] the faithful, but he was equal
[6] to the other Apostles. This is proved by arguments, and
[7] below, ch. 15. 846
[8] 2. In which it is proved that Peter was not
[9] the head and prince of the other
[10] Apostles, by the authorities of the teachers. 848
[11] 3. In which the second opinion is set out, concerning the inequality of the Apostles, namely that
[12] Christ appointed Blessed Peter the head
[13] and prince of the other Apostles.
[14] This is proved by two kinds of authority, which are answered, and the answers are refuted, ch. 4 and 5 through to ch. 10. 848
[15] 4. He attacks the response to the aforesaid authority of Christ. He proves most fully, by three arguments and many authorities of the saints, that the aforesaid words of Christ were directed to Peter in his own person and not to the others. 850
[16] 5. Where he answers the explanation of the aforesaid response, the teacher showing when the words of Christ spoken to one person are directed to others, and when they are not. 852
[17] 6. He responds to the remaining three allegations made in ch. 3 in support of the aforesaid response. 853
[18] 7. In which the authorities in the third allegation of ch. 3 are expounded. 854
[19] 8. Where the second response put forward in ch. 3 is attacked. 855
[20] 9. In which he responds to the authorities from the Apostle and the gloss adduced above in ch. 3 of this book. 856
[21] 10. Where the words of Christ spoken to Peter, "Feed my sheep," are discussed again. 857
[22] 11. In which the same words as in the preceding chapter are discussed again, the chapter being disapproved. 857
[23] 12. He proves Peter's primacy over the others by these words: "You are Peter, and upon this rock," etc. The contrary is argued by authorities and reasons, to which answer is given in ch. 18, 19 and 20. 859
[24] 13. The assertors of the opposite of what was said at the beginning of ch. 12 are refuted. 859
[25] 14. He treats the meaning of Christ's words, "You are Peter," etc., and how by them Christ handed over to Peter the principate over the other Apostles. 859
[26] 15. He proves the assumption of the preceding chapter, namely Peter's primacy, by the exposition of Anacletus. 860
[27] 16. The same, namely that he proves Peter's primacy, by the exposition of Marcellus and Cyprian. 861
[28] 17. He shows by which words Christ promised the primacy to Peter. 861
[29] 18. He solves the first argument adduced in ch. 12, by which it is proved that Peter's principate cannot be shown by those words, "You are Peter," etc. In solving it, he proves in many ways that not only Christ but also Peter was called the foundation of the Church. 861
[30] 19. He responds to some of the motives adduced in ch. 12. 862
[31] 20. He solves the remaining motives adduced in ch. 12. 863
[32] 21. He proves Peter's principate over the others by those words of Christ: "But I have prayed for you, Peter, that not," etc. 864
[33] 22. He proves Peter's primacy by a single argument, which is supported by many authorities of the saints. 864
[34] 23. He proves Peter's principate by a single argument, which is grounded in matters of faith and morals, and strengthens it by a single confirmation. 865
[35] 24. He attacks the double argument of the preceding chapter with a double argument, but responds to each argument. 866
[36] 25. He solves the arguments by which it is proved in ch. 1 that Christ did not appoint Peter over the other Apostles. 867
[37] 26. In which it is proved from authentic Scripture that Peter at one time used his power over the others. 868
