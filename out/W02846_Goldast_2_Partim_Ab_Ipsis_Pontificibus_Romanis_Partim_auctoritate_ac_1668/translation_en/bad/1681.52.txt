[1] And we see that judges who do something outside judicial process, in fact, and against whom force of arms may be resisted in fact, <i>l. prohibitum Cod. de iure fisc. lib. 10.</i> is noted by Innocent <i>in cap. si quando, de offic. ord. de leg.</i>; Bartolus in the tractate <i>qui sint rebelles</i>, in the word <i>rebellando, in princip.</i>; the gloss <i>in l. non videtur § qui iussu. Digest. de reg. iur.</i>; Dinus on the rule <i>quod quis, de regul. iuris in 6.</i>; Baldus <i>in l. ut vim in 7. quaest. ff. de vi. et iure in l. 1. in 12. quaest. Cod. unde vi.</i>; the Cardinal <i>in Clement. 1. in fine, in verbo 9. quaero, de celebr. Missa.</i>; Abbas <i>inc. Conquerente</i>, in the last notable point <i>de restit. spol.</i>; Alexander <i>in Consil. 144 post reddit. primum</i>; Conradus Colonensis, book 2, chapter 1, with similar authorities which are well known. So it seems that the same must be said of the supreme Pontiff: if he is caught in a notorious crime, by reason of which the Christian religion would be scandalized, he may and ought to be resisted, at least by this legal means, that he may be accused, so that the pernicious scandal which threatens may be removed.
