[1] We, as was fitting, have followed the ancient Fathers in this inquiry. First, Cyprian: <i>"Some people,"</i> he says, <i>"who have worked greatly and gloriously in the Church, have written to me and have imputed their work to the Lord, observing that saying of the Lord, WHEN YOU HAVE DONE ALL THINGS,"</i> etc. So that holy martyr, concerning the saints of the primitive Church. Second, Chrysostom, who says: <i>"It is written, WHEN YOU HAVE DONE ALL THINGS,"</i> etc. <i>"When therefore we give money to the needy, let us consider that God first made us obligated (or bound) to himself; but whatever we distribute is also his."</i> Third, Jerome. He says: <i>"This is the only perfection for men, if they know themselves to be imperfect: WHEN YOU HAVE DONE ALL THINGS, SAY, WE ARE UNPROFITABLE SERVANTS; WE HAVE DONE WHAT WE OUGHT TO DO."</i> Fourth, Augustine, on those words of Christ, John 15.14, <i>"YOU ARE MY FRIENDS, IF YOU DO WHAT I COMMAND YOU."</i> He says: <i>"O great goodness of God, to whom, since we owe obedience according to our condition, as servants to a Lord, subjects to a Powerful One, slaves to a Redeemer, he promises us the rewards of friendship, so that he may extort from us the obedience due from servants, so that he may invite by the promises of his benefits those whom he sees unwilling to serve of their own accord."</i> Fifth, Theophylact: <i>"WHEN YOU HAVE DONE ALL THINGS, etc. Even if someone has done all the commandments, he should not be exalted, for he has done nothing great; and woe to him unless he has done them."</i> Sixth, Bernard: <i>"WHAT WE OUGHT TO DO, WE HAVE DONE: for woe to us unless we had done it. This is virtue and the highest security, since indeed you live piously, and yet you attend more to what is lacking to you than to what you seem to have obtained."</i> Seventh, the testimony of Bede must not be passed over: <i>"If a man demands from a servant not a uniform but a manifold ministry, and yet does not thank him, how much more should you, who can do nothing without me, not weigh the merits of your labours by the length of time, but by love and voluntary service increase earlier things with ever new zeal! Therefore SAY, WE ARE UNPROFITABLE SERVANTS,"</i> etc. So he.
