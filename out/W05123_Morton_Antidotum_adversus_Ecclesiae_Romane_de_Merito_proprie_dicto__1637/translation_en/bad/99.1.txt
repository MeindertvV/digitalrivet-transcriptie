[1] We shall add Faber Stapulensis, Ferus, Stella, and three other famous authors of the Roman Church. Faber: <i>We believe that the reward is by no means owed to our works, but is the pure kindness of God: a man can owe grace to a man, as to an equal; but God can owe it to no one.</i> — <i>Far be it, therefore, that we should think that any merit is owed to us by God, however holily and religiously we have lived;</i> — <i>because we are UNPROFITABLE SERVANTS; nay, more than unprofitable, servants to whom nothing is owed, servants who always owe and can never pay, who can say,</i> WE HAVE DONE ONLY WHAT WE OUGHT TO HAVE DONE. So he; which did not at all please the Spanish Inquisitors; therefore they ordered it to be erased. Didacus Stella, who outshone almost all the preachers of his age, spoke freely and plainly: <i>we do not merit blessedness de condigno.</i> The same Inquisitors also wish this to be expunged. Joannes Ferus, second to no one else in the same faculty: <i>None of our works, of whatever kind, are meritorious, but God accepts them out of mercy.</i> And the Inquisitors order these words too to be smeared over with the same sponge; imitating, of course, those skilled surgeons who, by their purgings, draw off blood that is ever more corrupt.
