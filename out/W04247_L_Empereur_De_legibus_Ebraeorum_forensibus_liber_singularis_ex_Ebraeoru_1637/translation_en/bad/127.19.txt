[1] 1 In the original text it is פתח [patach], a word which in Holy Scripture is used only of seeing; though I do not doubt it can also be extended to hearing, since Isaiah 42:20 reads פתח אונים, "to open the ears." Likewise its synonym, the verb פתח, "he opened," is used more widely and is applied to speech too. So the Syriac word derived from it, ܐܬܦܬܚ [ethpattach], "be opened," refers to hearing and speaking in general, as in Mark 7:34, where by the command ears were opened and the bond of the tongue loosed. But the rabbinic teachers use the word ꝑρꝺ [the Hebrew root for "skilled"] very broadly, and so it denotes someone skilled, whether skilled in speaking, in reasoning, or in doing what befits an adult. Hence it is opposed here to deaf, foolish and minor. I think that is why in the Syriac language [illegible, about six letters] it is used for what is useful, convenient and fit. For the earlier sense is extended so that it marks not only ability and capacity for those earlier tasks, but whatever is suited to a business or thing can be said of it, [illegible, about five letters]: <i>to him convenient</i> and suitable or useful <i>was</i>. But in this place it is taken more strictly in our Mishnah. 2 Those who are opposed to them, the foolish, the deaf and the minors, are not liable if their ox gores another's ox. They give this reason in the case of minors: under the ancient law the senate would not have restitution made from their movable goods, but only from immovable ones (as was observed in the preceding passages). But the question concerns a gentle ox, from whose body the loss is repaid, as is clear from what came before. So if they made satisfaction for a gentle ox, that is, an <i>tam, innocent</i> (as they call it), the money would have to be taken from the price of the ox itself, that is, from movable goods; and this is contrary to the constitutions of the Jews. 3 But if a goring ox gores, they compare Exodus 21:29 with 36, and establish that since they are not of such capacity, and are not the kind of people to whom it suffices to announce that the ox is goring, the warning must be given in the presence of witnesses by an <i>Θπηρέτης</i> [attendant] (the word they use here). For here observe, if you wish to grasp the mind of the masters, that the earlier clause of the Mishnah and the later do not refer to the same ox: the one must be understood of the goring ox, the other of the gentle ox, as can also be drawn from the preceding annotation. 4 Moreover, what is added about restoration to the original state, namely, if those three classes of people recover and obtain their natural perfection, the ox is restored to its former state according to the judgment of certain persons; this is explained in the gloss: קסר רסות ... they hold (namely, the one whose judgment is given) that ownership changes the ox that has been convicted; that is, when this…
