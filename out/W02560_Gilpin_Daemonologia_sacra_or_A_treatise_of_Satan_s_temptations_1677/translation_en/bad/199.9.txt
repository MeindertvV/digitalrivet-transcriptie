[1] Satan thus builds his arguments on misrepresentations of God. A right view of God would easily answer them, but how hard it is for a person in an hour of temptation to dispel them by a right grasp of the ways of the holy God is plain from <i>Psal. 77.</i>, where the case of Asaph (or whoever he was) teaches us: 1. It is Satan's usual practice, in order to disquiet the hearts of God's children, to offer <i>a false prospect</i> of God. 2. This <i>overwhelms</i> their hearts with grief, <i>ver. 3.</i> 3. The more they persist in this method, under the <i>mists of prejudice</i>, the less they see, since they are apt to misconstrue everything in God to their own disadvantage: <i>ver. 3. I remembered God, and was troubled.</i> 4. The reason for all this trouble is that they can <i>only conclude</i> wrath and desertion from God's dealings toward them. 5. Until they look upon God <i>in another way</i>, and form better thoughts of him and his providences, even while those providences wear the appearance of severity, they can expect no relief from their complaints. For before the prophet got free of his trouble, he was forced to acknowledge <i>his mistake (ver. 10.)</i> in the misconstruction he had put upon God's dealings, and to resolve on entertaining better thoughts of God, <i>ver. 7.</i> His question, <i>Will the Lord cast off for ever, etc.?</i> indeed shows what he once thought, misled by Satan, but it also shows that he would never think so again: <i>Will the Lord cast off for ever?</i> Is this the voice of a despairing man? No, it is the voice of one who, through better information, has <i>rectified</i> his judgment, and is now resolved firmly to hold <i>the contrary</i> of what he thought before, as if he said, 'It is not possible that it should be so: he will not cast off for ever, and I will never again entertain such perverse thoughts of God.' 6. But before they can come to this, it will cost them some pains and serious thought. It is not easy to break these fetters and answer this argument. Yet those who would do so must <i>appeal</i> from their present feeling to a consideration <i>of the outcomes</i> of such dealings in <i>other persons</i>, or in themselves at <i>other times</i>. So the prophet, <i>ver. 5. considered the days of old, and the years of ancient times</i>; and <i>ver. 6.</i> he also drew on his own experience, calling <i>to remembrance</i> that after such dealings God had by his return of favour given him <i>songs in the night.</i>
