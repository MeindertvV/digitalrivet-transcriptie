[1] Yet we may note that, though Satan is usually forced to wait for the leisure of some men's timidity and bashfulness, and therefore cannot ripen error into an hasty birth as he desires (hence it is that one man often does no more than brew it, or perhaps only makes the rough draft of it, and another puts it forth and adorns it: for so it was between Lelius and Faustus Socinus, between David George and his successors), and though he is so confined to the first principles of error which he has instilled that he cannot at present extend them beyond their own just consequences; yet there are some choice principles of his which, if he can but fasten them upon the mind, at once open the gap to all kinds of errors imaginable. They are like the firing of a train of gunpowder, which in a moment blows up the whole fabric of truth: such are the delusions of <i>enthusiasm</i>, <i>inspirations</i> and <i>prophetic raptures</i>. Let these be once fixed, and then there is nothing so inhuman, irreligious, mad or ridiculous that Satan cannot with ease persuade men to it, and that also under the highest pretences of <i>religion</i> and <i>certainty</i>; the experience of all ages has made any further proof of this altogether needless.
