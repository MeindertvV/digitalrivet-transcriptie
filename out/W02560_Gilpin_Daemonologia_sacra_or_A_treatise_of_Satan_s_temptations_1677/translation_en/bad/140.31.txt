In this case Satan usually has greater <i>liberty to accuse</i>, and by his accusations he molests the conscience, because men of disturbed spirit, through the many evils that arise from it, <i>provoke God to desert</i> them and leave them in Satan's hand to be brought into an <i>hour</i> of temptation. Satan's commission is occasioned by our provocations, and the temptations arising from such a commission are usually dreadful. They are <i>solemn</i> temptations, and are called so in a singular manner; for I take those Scriptures to refer to these: <i>Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation</i>, Matt. 26. 41. <i>And lead us not into temptation</i>, Matt. 6. 13. Such temptations are not common ones, and their force and danger to the soul are unknown, whichever way they are designed, whether for sin or for terror. For several things <i>concur</i> in a solemn temptation. (1) Satan in a special manner <i>challenges</i> a man to the combat, or rather he <i>challenges</i> God to give him such a man to fight with, as he did concerning Job. Christ tells us of this in Luke 22. 31: Simon, <i>Satan hath desired to have you</i>. The word signifies a <i>challenging</i> or daring, and it seems the devil is often daring God to give us into his hand when we little know of it. (2) There is also a special suitableness of occasion and snare to the temper and state of men. Thus he took Peter at an advantage in the high priest's hall, and in the case we now speak of he takes advantage of men's provocations and melancholy. (3) There is always a <i>violent prosecution</i>, which our Saviour expresses under the comparison of sifting, which is a restless agitation of the corn, bringing what was at the bottom to the top and shuffling the top to the bottom, so that the chaff or dirt is always uppermost. (4) And to all this there is <i>divine permission</i>: Satan is let loose and we are left to our ordinary strength, as is implied in the expression, <i>He hath desired to have you, that he might sift you.</i> Now if the devil has such ground to give God a challenge concerning such men, and if God does (as he justly may) leave such men (whose bitterness of spirit has been <i>a smoke in his nostrils all the day</i>) in Satan's hand, he will so shake them that their consciences shall have no rest.
