[1] Secondly, it should also be observed that some errors bring with them <i>extraordinary, strange, unnatural, unusual actions</i>, and put men into such <i>odd postures</i>, attitudes and behaviours that it is easy to see they are moved by a force or power that is not <i>human</i>. Some have been carried to do things beyond anything that could be expected from the age and abilities of the persons concerned, such as ecstasies, trances and quakings in little children, and their prophesying and speaking threatenings from Scripture after such fits. Some have been driven into <i>ecstatic fury</i>, as Montanus was, of whom Eusebius bears witness: sometimes he would be seized by a kind of <i>malignant spirit</i>, and would suddenly break out into rage and madness, and immediately utter <i>rash and bold speeches, strange unusual voices, and prophesyings</i>; so that those who saw him judged him to be acted by the Devil. Others have been in a more <i>sober spiritual rapture</i>. I shall give you an instance of this from Mr. <i>Baxter</i>, in these words: <i>I have heard from an ancient godly man, who knew Arthington and Coppinger, that they were possessed with the spirit of the</i> Grundletonians. <i>The same man affirmed that he went among them only once himself, and after prayer they breathed on him, as though giving him the Holy Ghost; and he was so strangely transported for three days that he was not the same man, and his family wondered what was the matter with him. He made no confession of sin, but had an elevated strain in prayer, as if he had been in strange raptures; and after three days he was as before, and came no more to them.</i> Some have been carried into <i>childish</i> and ridiculous actions. Such was the behaviour of <i>Jo. Gilpin</i> in his delusion at <i>Kendal</i> in <i>Westmorland</i>: he went to the fiddler's house and played upon a bass viol as a token of spiritual melody; he crept up the streets on hands and knees as a token of bearing his cross; he made marks on the ground and beat it, as his mortification of sin; and a great many more things of the like nature.
