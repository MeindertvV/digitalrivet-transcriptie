He compares the <i>present state</i> of the person he deals with to the <i>highest</i> attainments and excellencies of Grace, allowing nothing to be Grace but what answers to the descriptions he has already given. Here the Tempter plainly makes use of a <i>false balance</i> and <i>a bag of deceitful weights.</i> For thus he puts it to them: <i>Thou sayest thou hast Grace, but thou dost altogether deceive thyself, for indeed thou hast none at all. Compare thyself with others who are noted in Scripture as undoubtedly gracious, and thou wilt see that in the balance thou art lighter than vanity.</i> Abraham <i>had Faith, but he believed beyond hope.</i> Moses <i>and</i> Paul <i>had love, but they showed it by preferring their brethren's happiness before their own.</i> David <i>was a saint, but he had a heart ravished with God. The martyrs spoken of in</i> Heb. 11. <i>could do wonders; they were above the fear of men, above the love of the world; they did not love their lives unto death. How joyfully did they take the spoiling of their goods? How courageously did they endure the sharpest torments?</i> Besides (saith he) <i>all the children of God are described as sanctified throughout, abounding in all the fruits of righteousness; their faith is working, their love still laborious, their hope produces steadfast patience. What art thou compared with these? That in thee which thou callest faith, or love, or patience, etc., is not fit to be named with these. Thy fears may tell thee that thou hast no faith, and so may thy works; thy murmurings under God's hand are sufficient evidence that thou hast no patience. The little that thou dost for God, or rather would do if it were not for thy own advantage, may convince thee that thou hast no love for him; thy weariness of services and duties, and thy confessed unprofitableness in all, proclaim that thou hast no delight in God, nor in his ways.</i> He further adds, to confirm all this: <i>Consider how far temporaries may go, who shall never go to heaven. Thou art far short of them; thy gifts, thy works, thy virtues, thy illumination, thy conversation, thy conscientiousness are nothing like theirs. How is it possible, then, that such a one as thou (a pitiful, contemptible creature) should have any true Grace in thee?</i> Thus he makes the application of the whole discovery of Grace which he has set before them. Though he need not urge <i>all</i> these things on everyone, any one of these particulars often serves his turn.
