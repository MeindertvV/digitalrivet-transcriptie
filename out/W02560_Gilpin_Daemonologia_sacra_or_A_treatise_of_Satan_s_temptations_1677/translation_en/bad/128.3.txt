[1] 4. <i>Crosses</i> and afflictions give Satan an opportunity to work upon our passions: disappointments of expectations, loss of friends, loss of estate, persecutions and sufferings for conscience' sake, and so on. None of these, in their own nature, <i>are joyous, but grievous;</i> and how much use they have been to the Devil in disturbing the minds of those who suffer is shown by common experience. The tears, sad countenances and doleful lamentations of men are true witnesses of the unrest of their hearts. Each person, pressed by the sense of his own pain, is ready to cry out: <i>Is there any sorrow like my sorrow? I am poor and comfortless; my lovers and my friends have forsaken me, and there is none to help.</i> Some grow faint under their burden, while their eyes fail from looking for relief, especially when new and unexpected troubles overwhelm their hopes: <i>When I looked for good, then evil came; and when I waited for light, there came darkness,</i> Job 30. 26. <i>Why hast thou smitten us, and there is no healing for us? We looked for peace, and there is no good; and for the time of healing, and behold trouble,</i> Jer. 14. 19. And here they sink, concluding that there is no hope.
