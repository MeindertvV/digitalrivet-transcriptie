[1] Now this is a great advantage to Satan. For first, by this means the <i>ignorant</i> multitude are often caught without any more ado; if they hear Scripture expressions they are apt to think that <i>all is truth</i> which is spoken <i>by them</i>; and they the rather believe it, because they imagine such teachers to be <i>well versed</i> in Scripture, and consequently either so <i>honest</i>, or so <i>knowing</i>, that they neither <i>can</i> nor <i>will</i> delude them. Secondly, there is a <i>majesty</i> in Scripture which (in some sense) doth <i>stick</i> to the very <i>expressions</i> of it. Men may perceive that generally hearers are more <i>affected</i> by <i>Scripture eloquence</i> than by <i>play-book language</i>; it has (as it were) a charm in the words, which makes the ear attentive, more than a quaint discourse, starched up in the dress of common rhetoric. One gives us an observation to that purpose from his own preaching, and many others may do so as well. While men hear such language, they have a reverence for it. And as physicians cover their pills with gold so that the patient may more willingly take them, so men often swallow down error without due consideration, because it is conveyed to them in a language which they respect.
