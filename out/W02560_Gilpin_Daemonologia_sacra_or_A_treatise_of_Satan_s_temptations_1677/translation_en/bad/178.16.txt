But most of all when our <i>natural fears are awakened.</i> For example, when a man has been under any great conviction, even if he is cured of his trouble, it usually leaves a weakness in the affected part, as bruises and injuries do in any member of the body. At a change of weather, or some other accidental hurt, the old trouble returns. Then, when fresh guilt begins to press hard on the conscience, not only do the broken bones ache again, through the <i>reviving</i> of former fears, but the impressions of his old suspicions, his bad opinion of himself, and his distrust of the deceitfulness of his heart, which had fixed themselves deeply, now make him <i>most fearful</i> of entertaining any <i>good thought</i> about himself. So if any consideration tending to his support is offered, he dares not come near it, suspecting that his greatest danger lies <i>on that hand.</i> Given these advantages, we should not think it strange that a child of God is driven into spiritual sadness, as some are, but should rather wonder that this is not the common condition of all Christians.
