[1] 2. Spiritual comfort is not only our satisfaction, but our <i>inward strength</i> and activity, for all holy services depend upon it. By this God strengthens our heart and girds up our loins <i>to run the ways of his commandments</i>; it also strengthens the soul to undergo <i>afflictions</i>, to glory in tribulations, to triumph in persecutions; the <i>outward man</i> is also fortified by the inward peace of the mind: <i>A merry heart doth good like a medicine, but a broken spirit drieth the bones</i>, Prov. 17. 22. All of which are intended by that expression, Neh. 8. 10. <i>The joy of the Lord is your strength</i>. It is strength to the body and to the mind, and that both for <i>service</i> and for <i>suffering</i>. The reason for this the Apostle hints at, Phil. 4. 7: <i>The peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds</i>. That is, peace guards us as with <i>a garrison</i> (for so the word implies), so that our affections (<i>our hearts</i>), being <i>entertained</i> with <i>divine satisfactions</i>, are not easily <i>enticed</i> by the baser offers of <i>worldly delights</i>, and our reasonings (<i>our minds</i>), being kept <i>steady</i> upon so <i>noble an object</i>, are not so easily <i>perverted</i> by the treacherous recommendation of vanities.
