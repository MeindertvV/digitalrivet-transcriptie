[1] Eighthly, in this scheme of false doctrine Satan is never altogether defeated. If he cannot thus defile their lives, yet it is a thousand to one that he <i>obstructs their graces</i> by it. What greater hindrance can there be to conversion than error? The word of truth is the means by which God, through his Spirit, begets us; it is part of that image of God which is implanted in us, and it is God's voice to the soul, to awaken it. It cannot then be imagined that God will give the honour of that work to any error, nor can truth take place or have its effect upon a soul <i>forestalled</i> by a contrary falsehood. Falsehood in possession will keep truth at the door. Conversion is hindered not only by those errors which directly contradict converting truths, but also by <i>collateral, non-fundamental errors</i>. These fill men's minds with <i>prejudice</i> against those who profess another persuasion, so that, for the sake of their beloved errors, men will not receive a warning or conviction from those who dissent from their opinions. They first count them <i>enemies</i>, and then they <i>despise</i> their message. It is no small advantage to Satan, in the great concern of conversion, to have such an obstruction at hand. Yet this is further useful to him, to hinder or weaken the graces of those already converted. If he can set God's children <i>madding</i> after error, or make them fond of <i>novelties</i>, he will by this means <i>exhaust</i> the vigour and strength of their hearts, so that the <i>substantials</i> of religion are neglected. For as hurtful plants <i>engross</i> all the moisture and fatness of the earth where they stand, and impoverish it so that it cannot nourish those of greater worth, so error takes possession of the <i>strength</i> of the spirit; and in the meantime neglected graces dwindle into emptiness and <i>fade as a leaf</i>. The most curious <i>questions and</i> opinions contribute nothing to the <i>establishment</i> of the heart; only grace does that. <i>Heb. 13.9. The heart is established with grace,</i> and not with disputes about <i>meats</i>. Nay, they do grace a prejudice, in that they make it <i>sick</i> and languishing; for to this sense is the <i>original</i> in <i>1 Tim. 6. 4.</i>, <i>doting about questions</i>, or growing diseased through the earnest pursuit of opinions.
