[1] That this is one of his grand stratagems can be shown well enough from the countless errors that claim <i>Scripture warrant.</i> Errors that rise above or go beyond Scripture, and so acknowledge no dependence on it, are few and rare. Indeed, among <i>Christians</i>, error can hardly thrive without some pretence of Scripture. Men would have enough trouble persuading <i>themselves</i> of such errors, but it would be impossible to form a <i>Party</i> or persuade others; such errors would at once be hissed out of the world. This is why <i>Atheism skulks</i> and hides itself, except where a generally tolerated profanity gives it more than the usual encouragement. This is not to be ascribed to any <i>shame-faced modesty</i> that <i>Atheists</i> could be supposed to nourish, but to the <i>general dislike</i> of others, who hold so firmly to the authority of the Bible that they reject every direct contradiction of it with great abhorrence. Hence also it is that some erroneous persons are forced into <i>contradictions</i> in their <i>practice</i> against their professed <i>principles</i>, because they find it impossible to spread their errors without some pretence or other to Scripture. Those who would undermine those sacred records are forced to make use of their authority to prove what they say. The <i>Papists</i> have a <i>quarrel</i> with them, and envy them the title of <i>Perfection</i> and <i>Perspicuity</i>, intending to <i>introduce traditions</i> and to set up the <i>Pope's</i> judicial authority in matters of faith. And when they have said all they can to subject the Scriptures to the <i>Pope's</i> determination, they are at last forced to depend on the <i>Scriptures</i> to prove the <i>Pope's</i> determination. They would prove the <i>Scriptures</i> by the <i>Church</i>, and then the <i>Church</i> by the <i>Scriptures</i>. This is a <i>circle</i> they have often been told of, and some of the wiser sort among themselves are ashamed of it. Others also, who will not allow the Scriptures to be a general standing rule, are yet forced to make it, in some cases, a <i>rule to themselves</i>, and eagerly plead that it be so for <i>others</i>. Those who pretend to be <i>above ordinances</i>, and decry <i>outward teachings</i> as unnecessary or harmful, nevertheless teach outwardly, because they see they cannot enlarge the <i>empire</i> of error without such teaching. Those very errors that make it their chief business to render the Scriptures no better than <i>an old almanack</i> still appeal to Scripture to countenance their blasphemous assertions. If they get any scrap or shred of it that, by their <i>unjust torture</i>, can be wrested to say any such thing, or anything toward it, they think all their follies are thereby <i>patronized</i>. Commonly such men either fix upon those passages that point out the necessary <i>concomitants</i> of the Spirit and the heart with the outward <i>act</i> of service, and from there, separating what God has joined together, they set up <i>spiritual</i> sabbaths, <i>spiritual</i> baptism, and <i>spiritual</i> worship, to cry down and cashier the <i>external acts</i> of such ordinances; or they claim <i>kinship</i> with Scripture, as though prophesying or foretelling those <i>new</i> administrations which they <i>are about to set up</i>. Let <i>H. Nicholas</i> be an instance of this. Though he decried the service of the Law under God the Father, and the service of the Belief under Christ, and in the room of both would set up another administration under the Spirit, yet (so that he might be the better believed) he applied several Scriptures to his purpose, as though they prophetically foretold <i>H. Nicholas</i> and his services. He would have men imagine that he was <i>that angel flying in the midst of heaven with the everlasting Gospel</i>, Rev. 14. 6. <i>And that prophet enquired after by the Jews</i>, Joh. 1. 21. <i>Art thou that prophet, and that man ordained to judge the world?</i> Act. 17. 31. And that the times of his dispensation were the times of perfection and glory spoken of in 1 Cor. 13. 9. and Heb. 6. 1. <i>Saltmarsh</i>, and several others, had the like pretences for new administrations.
