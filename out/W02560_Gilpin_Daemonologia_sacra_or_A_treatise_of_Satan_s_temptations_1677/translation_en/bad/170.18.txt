[1] This kind of trouble takes in almost every kind of action: it extends to a man's <i>calling</i>, the way he manages it, the rates he charges, and the prices he gives for his goods; our every <i>natural</i> action of sleeping, eating, drinking, company and recreation is not exempt. In all of these the Devil frightens the timorous conscience that (it may be) he has offended: if he buys or sells, he is disquieted by a <i>perhaps</i> that he has sold too dear or bought too cheap; if he eats or sleeps, he fears he has been excessive, a sluggard or a glutton. Thus some men are troubled in everything they do.
