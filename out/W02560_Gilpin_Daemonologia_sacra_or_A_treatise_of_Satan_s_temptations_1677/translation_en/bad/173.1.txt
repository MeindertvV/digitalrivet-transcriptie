[1] 2. Another degree of trouble arising from an evil conscience is when the <i>condition</i> of a regenerate person is judged by conscience (but falsely) to be <i>very bad.</i> I must here (as some others have done), for want of better terms, distinguish between the <i>state</i> of regeneracy and a man's <i>condition</i> in that state, though the words [<i>state</i> and <i>condition</i>] are used interchangeably, the one for the other. A man may be in a <i>regenerate state</i>, and yet his condition in <i>that state</i> may be very bad and blameworthy, as when he does not walk worthy of so holy a calling; just as a person may be a man and yet be unhealthy or languishing. Thus many of the <i>Asian</i> churches were <i>true</i> churches, and yet in a <i>bad</i> condition: some were <i>lukewarm</i>; some <i>had a name to live</i>, <i>and yet were</i> comparatively <i>dead</i>, because <i>their works</i> were not full or <i>perfect</i> before God; and others had <i>left their first</i> love. To this purpose is that of the Apostle, <i>2 Cor. 13. 5. Know ye not your own selves, how that Jesus Christ is in you, except ye be Reprobates?</i> where the word <i>Reprobate</i> is not to be taken in the strict, severe sense of one not <i>elected</i>, but of one whose conversation is not <i>so sound</i> and approved as it should be; for this relates not to their <i>being in Christ</i>, but to their <i>assurance</i> of being in that state, which the Apostle affirms they might know, unless the fault lay in their negligent and careless conversation.
