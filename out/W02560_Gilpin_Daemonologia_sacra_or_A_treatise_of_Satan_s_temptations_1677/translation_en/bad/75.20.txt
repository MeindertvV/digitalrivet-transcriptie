[1] Eighthly, Satan sometimes pleads for error from the ease, peace, or other advantages which men claim they have received since they engaged in such a way or took up such a persuasion. This is an argument from the effect, and it is often used to confirm men in their opinions. Hence they satisfy themselves with these reasonings: "I was always before under fears and uncertainties; I never had peace or rest in my mind. I tried several courses and followed several parties, but I never had satisfaction or comfort until now, and by this I know that I am in the right way." Others argue in the same manner from their abundance and outward prosperity: "I met with nothing but crosses and losses before, but now God has blessed me with an increase of substance, and has prospered my trade and undertakings," and so on. These, though they are plainly weak and deceitful grounds, are held to be strong and conclusive by those who have first made up their minds to an error. For men are so eager to justify themselves in what they have undertaken that they greedily catch at anything that has the least appearance of probability to serve their ends.
