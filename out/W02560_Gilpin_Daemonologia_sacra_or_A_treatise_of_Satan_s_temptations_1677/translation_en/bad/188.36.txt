[1] 1. As to his <i>sophistry</i>, by which he argues the children of God into a wrong apprehension of themselves, it is very great. He has a wonderful dexterity in framing arguments against their peace; he has a variety of shrewd objections and subtle answers to the usual replies by which they seek to beat him off. There is not a <i>fallacy</i> by which a cunning <i>sophister</i> would seek to entangle his adversary in disputation that Satan would not make use of, as I might show you particularly if it were proper for a common audience. Though he has so much impudence that he does not blush at the most <i>silly</i> and <i>contemptible</i> reason that can be offered, he also has so much wit as to urge arguments that are never <i>true</i>, yet <i>always probable</i>. How much he can <i>prevail</i> upon the beliefs of men in matters relating to their <i>souls</i> may be conjectured from the success he has upon the <i>understandings</i> of men, when he argues them into <i>error</i> and makes them <i>believe a lie</i>. We usually say, and rightly, that Satan cannot in any case <i>force</i> us properly to consent; yet, considering the advantages he takes, the ways he has to <i>prepare</i> the hearts of men for his impressions, and his very great <i>subtlety</i> in disputing, we may say that he can so order the matter that he will <i>seldom miss</i> his aim. It would be an endless work to gather up all the arguments Satan has used to prove the condition or state of God's children to be bad.
