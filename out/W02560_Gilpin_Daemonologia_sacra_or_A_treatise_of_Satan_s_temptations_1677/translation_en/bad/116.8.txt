[1] 2. That they are <i>unfruitful</i>. This is our own complaint, for troubled spirits commonly have <i>great expectations</i> from duties <i>at first</i>. They run to them, as the impotent and sick people ran to the Pool of Bethesda, with thoughts of <i>immediate</i> ease as soon as they step into them. But when they have tried, and waited a while, stretching themselves upon duty as Elisha's servant laid the staff upon the face of the Shunammite's son, and yet there is <i>no voice</i> nor hearing, no answer from God, no peace, then they are presently dissatisfied. They reflect on the promises of God and the counsels of good men, saying, <i>Where is all the pleasantness you speak of? What advantage is it that we have run and laboured thus, when we have got nothing?</i> Then it is easy for the Devil to add, <i>And why do you wait on the Lord any longer?</i> 3. His last and most dangerous argument is that they are <i>sinful</i>. Unfitness for duty produces many distractions, much deadness, wandering thoughts, great interruptions and pitiful performances. Hence the troubled soul comes away from duty <i>wounded</i> and <i>halting</i>, more distressed when he has finished than when he began. Upon these considerations, he judges that all his service was <i>sin</i>, <i>a mocking of God</i>, <i>a taking of his name in vain</i>, nay a very <i>blasphemous affront</i> to the divine Majesty. Upon this the Devil puts the question to his heart: <i>whether it be not better to forbear all duty</i>, <i>and to do nothing?</i> Thus Satan makes the trouble of the mind serve him, and often with the success he intends. For a dejected spirit not only supplies the <i>materials</i> of the weapons which the Devil forges against it, but is also much prepared to <i>receive</i> them into its own bowels. It <i>grants</i> the grounds of these arguments, and the <i>inferences</i> are commonly <i>consented</i> to, so that ordinarily duty is neglected either 1. through <i>sottishness</i> of heart, or 2. through <i>frightful</i> fears, or 3. through <i>desperateness</i>, bringing a man to the very brink of that <i>atheistical</i> resolve, <i>I have cleansed my hands in vain.</i>
