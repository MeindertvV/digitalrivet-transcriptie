[1] First, in things <i>clearly</i> demonstrated to the understanding, though the will cannot <i>directly</i> oppose them, nor prevail to have them judged false, it can <i>indirectly</i> hinder the work of the understanding and divert it from fixing its <i>consideration</i> upon the truth, or from working itself into <i>positive determinations</i> that would bring the truth into practice. Thus many people, who cannot but believe that there is a God and that his law is true, are biased by their lusts, the power of pleasures, or self-interest, and so prevail upon their understandings to take up <i>other objects</i> of consideration. They are therefore said to forget God and to cast his commandments behind their backs, and not to remember their latter end, though they cannot but believe that they shall die. Truth may be <i>imprisoned</i> and fettered where it cannot be <i>slain</i>. We read of <i>holding the truth in unrighteousness</i>, Rom. 1.18. That was the case with those <i>Heathens</i> (of whom the Apostle speaks): because of their vicious inclinations and practices, though they could not obliterate the notions of equity and religion imprinted on their minds, they kept them down, as captives in a dungeon, and did not allow them to rise up into a just practical improvement. Now the wrong done to truth in this way is not only that it is rendered <i>unfruitful</i> and useless for the present, but also that the Devil gains <i>time</i> by which to gather <i>more</i> forces against that truth. By frequent onsets of <i>contrary</i> arguings, especially taking advantage of the mind's <i>indifference</i> and remissness, begotten by long and frequent diversions, he puts <i>another face</i> upon it and by degrees overturns former persuasions. This was the very case of the <i>Heathens</i> in the place just cited. Being first <i>swayed</i> by their impieties, they became unwilling to yield to those dictates of light and justice which they had. Having thus gratified their lusts, and the Devil further prevailing upon them to find <i>evasions</i> from the power of those truths, they began to draw <i>unsuitable inferences</i> from premises they could not deny, and so became <i>sottish</i> and <i>vain</i> in their reasonings, <i>changing the glory of the incorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man.</i> By such practices against the truth they at last <i>changed the truth of God into a lie</i>, vers. 25, and at length <i>obliterated</i> the knowledge of God from their minds. This is Satan's old method of overturning the truth at last: he diverts the mind from receiving the present powerful impressions of those principles.
