[1] That the <i>lives</i> and <i>practices</i> of men are so affected by <i>corrupt doctrines</i> may be plain to anyone who is at all acquainted with Scripture or history. The Apostle <i>Paul</i> tells us that <i>faith</i> and <i>conscience</i> are so bound together that they live and die together, and that when the one is <i>shipwrecked</i>, the other is <i>drowned</i> along with it. In <i>Phil. 3. 2.</i> he seems severely harsh towards those of the <i>concision</i>. He calls them <i>dogs</i>: <i>beware of dogs</i>; <i>beware of evil workers</i>. I think the reason for this expression lies less in these resemblances: that dogs <i>spoil</i> the flock by <i>devouring</i> it, or that they are <i>fawning</i> creatures, or that they are <i>industrious</i> in pursuing their prey. (In all these respects false teachers may be compared to dogs, for they spare not the flock, they compass sea and land to gain disciples, and they entice them with fair speeches.) Rather, he means the comparison to express the <i>profane life</i> and conduct of these seducers, for dogs are <i>filthy</i> creatures, as the proverb has it: <i>the dog returns to his vomit</i>. And common prostitutes, on account of their uncleanness, were called <i>dogs</i> in the Old Testament. So some explain <i>Deut. 23. 18.</i>: <i>the hire of a whore</i>, or the price of a dog. And we have full and clear descriptions of seducers drawn from their wicked and abominable practices. <i>2. Pet. 2. 10.</i> <i>They that walk after the flesh, in the lust of uncleanness, and despise government; presumptuous are they, self-willed, and they are not afraid to speak evil of dignities.</i> Vers. 14: <i>Having eyes full of adultery, and that cannot cease from sin; an heart exercised with covetous practices; cursed children.</i> Vers. 18: <i>They allure through the lusts of the flesh, through much wantonness.</i> — <i>Jude</i> vers. 4: <i>There are certain men crept in unawares, who were before of old ordained to this condemnation; ungodly men, turning the grace of God into lasciviousness.</i> Vers. 16: <i>These are murmurers, complainers, walking after their own lusts,</i> etc. <i>2. Tim. 3.</i> vers. 2, 3, 4, 5: <i>Men shall be lovers of their own selves; covetous, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, unholy, without natural affection, truce-breakers, false accusers, incontinent, fierce, despisers of those that are good, traitors, heady, highminded, lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God; of this sort are they which creep into houses.</i> — All of these set forth heretical persons as the most scandalous and wicked wretches we shall meet with: grossly filthy in themselves, corrupt in all the duties of their relations, both natural and civil, and defiled in all their dealings with men.
