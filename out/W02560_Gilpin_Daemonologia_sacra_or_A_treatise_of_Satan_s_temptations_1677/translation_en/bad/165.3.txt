[1] 1. That a man has nothing dearer to him than his soul. Alas, that cannot be counterbalanced by the gaining of the whole world; and to have no hope or expectation of its salvation must needs be terribly frightening. 2. When these suspicions of non-election prevail, all promises and comforts are urged in vain, and such people commonly return them to those who offered them, with this reply: "They are true, and useful to those to whom they belong, but they do not belong to me." Nay, all means are rejected as useless. If such people are advised to pray or to read, they will in their fit of terror refuse everything, on this ground: "They are not elected." And then to what purpose (they say) is prayer, or any endeavour? "For who can alter his decree?" And indeed, if their terrors continued at a height without intermission, they would never do anything; but this is their help, that some secret underground hopes, which they do not notice, revive at least sometimes and move them to endeavours, which (through God's blessing) become means of better information. 3. Though Satan's injections of non-election are altogether unprovable, and withal so terrifying that one might suppose men would not be quick to believe in so great a misfortune, yet he can prevail so far that the persons named above (especially the melancholy) are made to believe him, and this chiefly by possessing their imaginations with his frequent, confident affirmations. We see it is a common practice to teach birds musical notes and sounds, which is achieved only by constant repetition, until a strong impression is made upon their fancy. And thus one man may impose upon the imagination of another with his songs or sayings, for what we hear often we cannot help repeating in our thoughts, being strongly fixed upon our fancy. No wonder, then, if Satan, by often repeating, "Thou art not elected, thou art damned," etc., forms so strong an impression upon the imagination that poor amazed creatures learn to say after him, and then take the echoes of their fancy to be the voice of conscience condemning them. Now then, if the unhappiness suspected be the greatest beyond all comparison, if these suspicions entertained cut off all the succours of comfort that may arise from the promises of God and the endeavours of man, and if Satan can prevail with men to entertain them with any persuasion (as we see he can), how dreadfully will these persuasions recoil upon a man! And thus will his thoughts run: "I am persuaded I am not elected; and if not elected, then comforts and prayers are all in vain; and if these be in vain, there is no possibility of salvation, nor the least hope of a 'who knows' or a 'peradventure'; and if that, oh, unspeakably miserable!"
