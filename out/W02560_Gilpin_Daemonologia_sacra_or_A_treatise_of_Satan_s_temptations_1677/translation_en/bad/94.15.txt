[1] If a stranger of another religion comes among them, then, as their first essay, they show him all their playthings, so that his affections may be tickled by the outward pomp and ornament of their way. For they know by experience that a <i>glittering outside</i> and <i>a great deal of ado</i> in bodily labour is the whole of most men's religion; if it has <i>body</i> enough, they never inquire whether it has <i>spirit</i> or life within. A dead <i>carcass in robes</i>, that puts them to the exercise of their <i>postures</i> and ceremonious compliments, makes up a <i>more agreeable</i> religion for a carnal man than a <i>living</i>, <i>spiritual</i> service, which necessarily puts them upon <i>inward</i> care and watchfulness in the <i>constant</i> exercise of holy <i>spiritual</i> graces, without affording any considerable <i>gratification</i> to the senses. Hence it is truly more difficult (and yet inwardly more beautiful and glorious) to <i>pray in faith and humility</i>, even in short breathings after God, than to say a <i>thousand Ave Marias</i>, or to perform a task of ordinary <i>penance</i>. But as those who have no children of their own delight themselves in playing with a <i>monkey</i> or <i>baboon</i>, so those who do not know how to worship God <i>in spirit and truth</i> seek to satisfy themselves in the performance of <i>external gesture</i> and ceremony.
