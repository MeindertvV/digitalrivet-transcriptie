[1] 2. Another piece of his sophistry is his improving certain <i>false notions</i> (which Christians of the weaker sort have received) into <i>proofs</i> of their unregenerate or bad condition. As there are <i>vulgar errors</i> concerning natural things, so there are <i>popular errors</i> concerning spiritual things. These mistakes, for the most part, have their origin in the fancies or misapprehensions of <i>unskilful</i> men. Some have perhaps been preached and taught as truths; others have arisen, without any teacher, from sheer ignorance, being the conclusions and surmises which weak heads have formed for themselves from the sayings or practices of men, which were either not <i>cleared</i> of the danger of mistake, or not <i>distinctly</i> enough apprehended as was necessary. Once these false inferences are set on foot, they are <i>traditionally</i> handed down to others, and in time they gain among the simple the reputation of undoubted truths. Now wherever Satan finds any of these that suit his purpose (for whatever mistake we entertain, he will be sure at one time or another to throw it in our way), he will make it the foundation of an argument against the one who has received it, and that with very great advantage. For a falsehood in the <i>premises</i> will usually produce a falsehood in the <i>conclusion</i>. And these falsehoods being taken for granted, the Devil does not trouble himself to prove them; if he can but exactly fit them to something in the person which cannot be denied, he at once carries the cause and condemns him by his <i>own concessions</i>, as out of his <i>own mouth</i>.
