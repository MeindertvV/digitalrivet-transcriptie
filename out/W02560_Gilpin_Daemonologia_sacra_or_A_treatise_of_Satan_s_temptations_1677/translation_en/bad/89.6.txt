[1] The burdensome heap of ceremonious <i>superstitions</i> in Popery was the work of <i>several ages</i>; they were not brought in all at once. One man, in a devotional heat, fancied such a ceremony a fit testimony of zeal, or a proper stirring of his affections. Another devised a second, and so on all along. As men's minds were <i>best pleased</i> with their <i>own inventions</i>, and as those inventions had enough <i>credit</i> or authority to recommend them to others, they increased the sum by <i>new additions</i>, until at last they became a burden not to be borne. And still, as they receded from the <i>primitive purity</i> and became more careless and corrupt in their lives (for from good bishops they declined to merely <i>tolerable archbishops</i>, until at last they became <i>incurable Babylonians</i>), so they departed gradually from the simplicity of the Gospel and <i>abounded</i> in contrivances of ceremonies.
