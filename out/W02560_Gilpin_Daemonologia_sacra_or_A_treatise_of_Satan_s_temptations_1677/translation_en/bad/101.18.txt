[1] Now these <i>Dæmons</i>, they supposed, were the <i>souls of dead Men</i> who had been more than ordinarily famous in their generation. Thus <i>Ninus</i> made an image to his father <i>Belus</i> after he was dead, and caused him to be worshipped. <i>Hermes</i> confesses that <i>Æsculapius</i>, grandfather to <i>Asclepius</i>, and <i>Mercury</i>, his own grandfather, were worshipped as gods of <i>this</i> order. I might produce abundant instances to this purpose, but to go on: these <i>Dæmons</i>, because the care of <i>Terrestrial</i> affairs was committed to them (as <i>Celsus</i> argues against <i>Origen</i>), and because of the help and advantage that men might receive from them, they supposed it to be <i>gratitude</i> and duty to <i>worship</i> them, and this worship they performed at their <i>Images Sepulchres</i> and <i>Reliques</i>. To this purpose <i>Plutarch</i> tells us of the bones of Theseus, and <i>Plato</i> of the shrines [θήκαι] of their <i>Dæmons</i>.
