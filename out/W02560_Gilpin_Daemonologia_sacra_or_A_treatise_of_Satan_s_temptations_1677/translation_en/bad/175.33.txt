[1] 2. There is also no difference in the <i>degree</i> of the trouble: a child of God may be handled with as much apparent <i>severity</i> as the one whom God intends for a future Tophet. 3. Nor is there any such notable difference in the <i>working</i> of the spirits of the one and the other that <i>they</i> themselves at present, or <i>others</i> who stand by, can <i>easily</i> observe. Yet a formal difference there is, for grace in the heart of the one will reveal its life in some <i>breathing</i> or <i>Pulse</i>. And though sometimes it acts so faintly or confusedly that <i>God only</i> can distinguish it, yet often those who are <i>experienced</i> observers will discern some real breathings after God, and true loathing of sin, and other traces of faith and love, which are not so discernible to the parties themselves. 4. But in <i>God's design</i> the difference is very great: the wicked lie under his lash as <i>Malefactors</i>, but the regenerate are as patients <i>under Cure</i>, or children <i>under Discipline</i>. 5. And accordingly the <i>Issue</i> determines that God's intention in wounding their spirits was not alike for both: the one, at last, coming out of the furnace as <i>Gold</i>, the other still remaining as <i>reprobate Silver</i>, or being consumed as dross. Thus have you seen the nature and degree of spiritual sadness.
