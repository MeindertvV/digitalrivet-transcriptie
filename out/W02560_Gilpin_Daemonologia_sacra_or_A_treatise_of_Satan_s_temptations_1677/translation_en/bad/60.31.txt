[1] Fourthly, because religious holiness has a beauty in it and is very lovely, the devil does all he can to fill men with the highest <i>reverence</i> for these <i>pretences</i> of religious strictness. Those who will not take the <i>pains</i> to practise them can still show an excessive respect and <i>admiration</i> for those who have grown <i>famous</i> in the use of such things. By that means, being almost adored, they are without doubt persuaded that everything they teach or do is right, and in a doting fondness they multiply superstitious errors. <i>Idolatry</i> is thought to have a great part of its origin in this. While men tried to express their grateful and admiring <i>remembrances</i> of excellent persons by setting up their <i>pictures</i>, their posterity began to worship them as <i>gods</i>. Pilgrimages were first set on foot by the respect men gave to <i>places</i> made <i>famous</i> by persons and deeds of more than ordinary holiness. And because the devil found men so very apt to please themselves in such devotional <i>reverences</i>, he worked on their superstitious humour to <i>multiply</i> the <i>occasions</i> for them. By fabulous traditions he sent them to places made <i>memorable</i> by nothing but dreams and <i>impostures</i>. You may see much of this if you accompany a <i>caravan</i> from <i>Cairo</i> to <i>Mecca</i> and <i>Medina</i>. There you would see zealous pilgrims with many orisons and prayers <i>compassing</i> the house of <i>Abraham</i>; <i>kissing</i> a stone which (they are told) fell from heaven; blessing themselves with a <i>relic</i> of the old vesture of Abraham's house; <i>washing</i> themselves in the pool which (so their tradition goes) the <i>angel</i> showed to <i>Hagar</i>; <i>saluting</i> the Mountain of Pardons; throwing <i>stones</i> in defiance of the devil, as (their legend tells them) <i>Ishmael</i> did; saying their prayers on the <i>Mountain of Health</i>; visiting the <i>Prophet's tomb</i> at <i>Medina</i>, and so on. The like you might observe among the <i>Papists</i> in their pilgrimages to <i>Jerusalem</i> and the <i>Sepulchre</i>, to the chapel of the <i>Lady</i> of <i>Loreto</i>, and to other places. By such devices as these, unobservant people are carried away with a <i>pleasure</i>, so that they not only persuade themselves they are <i>very devout</i> in these reverences, but they also become <i>unalterably fixed</i> to the errors that support these <i>delightful</i> practices, or that follow from them as consequences.
