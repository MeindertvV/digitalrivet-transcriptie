[1] 1. That these occasions, meeting with such dispositions, do naturally raise great disturbances. This is evident from what has been said already: for (1.) all these dispositions carry as much <i>fire</i> in their own bosoms as is sufficient to burn up the standing corn of any man's peace. What is anger, but an <i>inward burning</i>, a restless confusion of the spirits, sometimes a frenzy, a distraction, a troubled sea full of rage, a wild beast let loose? Envy, which is a <i>fretful</i> peevishness, a vexatious repining, needs no other tormentors than its own <i>furies</i>, recoiling upon him who bred it, because it cannot vent its spite upon its objects. An envious person is a <i>self-murderer</i> by the verdict of Eliphaz, Job 5.
