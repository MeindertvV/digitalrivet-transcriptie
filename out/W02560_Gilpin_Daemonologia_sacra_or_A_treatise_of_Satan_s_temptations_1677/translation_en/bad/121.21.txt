[1] 9. Let it also be reckoned among the advantages which Satan has against men from trouble of spirit that it is a <i>contentment</i> to him to see them in their miseries. It is a <i>sport</i> to him to see them (as <i>Job</i> speaks) take their flesh in their teeth and cry out in the bitterness of their souls. Their groanings are his music. When they wallow in ashes, drown themselves in tears, roar till their throat is dry, and stretch out their hands for help, then he <i>gluts</i> his heart in looking upon their woes. When they fall upon God with their unjust surmises and evil interpretations of Providence, questioning his favour, denying his grace, wishing they had never been born, then he <i>claps</i> his hands and shouts <i>a victory.</i> The pleasantest sight to him is to see <i>God hiding</i> himself from his child, and that child <i>broken</i> with fears, <i>torn</i> in pieces with griefs, made a <i>brother to dragons</i>, a <i>companion</i> to <i>owls</i>, under <i>restless anxieties</i>, perpetual <i>lamentations</i>, <i>feeble</i> and sore broken, their strength <i>dried</i> like a potsherd, their <i>throat</i> dry, their <i>tongue</i> cleaving to their jaws, their <i>bowels boiling</i>, their <i>bones</i> burnt with heat, their <i>skin</i> black upon them, their <i>flesh</i> consumed, their <i>bones</i> sticking out, <i>chastened</i> with strong pain upon their bed. This is one of Satan's delightful spectacles, and to these ends he does all he can to bereave them of their comfort. We may the more certainly persuade ourselves that this is true when we consider the grounds mentioned above: his <i>malicious nature</i>, the <i>advantages</i> of spiritual peace, and the <i>disadvantages</i> of spiritual trouble.
