[1] Job's troubles were very great, and his case was extraordinary. Satan had maliciously stripped him of all outward comforts, and he bore this with admirable patience: <i>Job 1.21. Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the Lord gave, and the Lord hath taken away; blessed be the name of the Lord.</i> The Devil, seeing himself now defeated, obtains a new commission, by which Job is wholly put into his hand (only his life excepted, <i>chap. 2.9.</i>). He sets upon him again, and in this new encounter labours to bring upon him spiritual distresses. He accordingly uses his losses and sufferings to that end, as appears from his endeavours and their success. For just as he tempted him <i>by his wife</i> to a desperate <i>disregard</i> of God, who had so afflicted him, saying <i>Curse God and die</i>, so he also tempted him <i>by his friends</i> to <i>question</i> the state of his soul and his integrity, and all from the consideration of his outward miseries. To this purpose all their discourses are directed. Eliphaz, <i>chap. 4. 5, 6, 7.</i>, from Job's sufferings and his conduct under them, takes occasion to jeer at his former piety, as being nothing but feigned: <i>It is come upon thee, and thou faintest: is not this thy fear, thy confidence, thy hope, and the uprightness of thy ways?</i> That is, is all your religion come to this? He also concludes him to be wicked: <i>Who ever perished, being innocent? and where were the righteous cut off?</i> Bildad, <i>chap. 8. 6, 13.</i>, charges him with hypocrisy on the same ground, and while Job makes his defence, Zophar plainly gives him the lie, <i>chap. 11. 3.</i> And at this rate they go their round. All this while Satan, whose design it was to afflict his conscience with the sense of divine wrath, <i>secretly</i> joins in with these accusations, so that although <i>Job</i> stoutly defended his <i>Integrity</i>, he was nevertheless wounded with inward <i>distresses</i>, and concluded that these dealings of God against him were no less than God's severe observance of his iniquity. This is plain from his bemoaning himself in <i>chap. 10. 2. I will say unto God, Do not condemn me; shew me wherefore thou contendest with me. Verse 16, 17. Thou huntest me as a fierce lion; thou renewest thy witnesses against me, etc.</i>
