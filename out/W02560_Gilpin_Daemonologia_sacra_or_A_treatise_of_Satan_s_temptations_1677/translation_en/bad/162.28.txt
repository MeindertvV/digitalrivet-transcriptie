[1] 2. In this too he <i>watches for his advantages</i>. Some men are so unhappily suited to his purpose that Satan succeeds with them more easily than with others. He usually fixes his eyes, (1.) on <i>young people</i> at their <i>first</i> earnest attention to, and reflection on, the truths of Scripture. Their hearts are then <i>tender</i>. (Youth has a natural tenderness of heart; we find the two joined in the character of <i>Rehoboam</i>, <i>2 Chron. 13.7.</i>: When Rehoboam <i>was young, and tender-hearted</i>.) They are quick to receive strong impressions. When those who were formerly careless of their spiritual welfare begin to be serious, they no sooner fall upon the consideration of those weighty doctrines, that there are <i>sheep</i> and <i>goats</i>, some <i>saved</i> and some <i>damned</i>; that the elect are <i>few</i> compared with the <i>many</i> who take the broad way to destruction; and that these were <i>ordained unto</i> life from eternity, and <i>these only</i>, and so on, than Satan is ready with this suspicion: <i>And what do you know, but that you are one of these excluded wretches? If only a few are saved, is it not a thousand to one that you are none of them? For why should God look upon you more than upon another?</i> These are his first attempts upon young men beginning to be serious, and afterwards he proceeds with greater boldness as he sees occasion. (2.) He also does this to persons who are <i>somehow quickened</i> to a devout fear of God and a care for their souls, but who are at the same time <i>ignorant</i>, and not able <i>clearly</i> to grasp, or <i>orderly</i> to arrange, the doctrines of Scripture into a due <i>consistency</i> with one another. Their <i>anxious fears</i> lead them to inquire what God has said concerning the everlasting state of men; and before they are able to <i>digest</i> the principles of religion, Satan thrusts some truths <i>edgeways</i> against them, which throw them into great terror, while (through their ignorance) other truths, appointed and declared for the comfort of those who hunger and thirst after righteousness, cannot come in to their relief. How frightening the truths of God's election must be when they stand forth <i>alone</i>, not accompanied by the <i>invitations</i> of the gospel, which promise <i>pardon</i> and <i>acceptance</i> to all who will come in and submit to Christ! Satan usually keeps such men fixed upon those truths which have the most <i>dismal aspect</i>, and while they are held there, they can draw no other conclusions than these: <i>That they are in danger, and for all they know, utterly lost.</i> (3.) Satan also has this plot against those who, by some <i>grievous</i> wickedness, or by a <i>long continuance</i> in sin, have greatly provoked the Lord. Here he uses arguments drawn from the heinousness of their iniquity: you are a <i>reprobate</i>, because you have committed these <i>great evils</i>; these are <i>marks</i> of damnation, and so on. Although these arguments are of <i>no value</i>, and in no way prove what they are brought to prove, yet Satan <i>injects suspicions</i>, and meanwhile their own consciences justly accuse them, so that they sink under their fear, allow Satan to draw <i>whatever conclusion</i> he will, and then they <i>sign their names</i> to it. (4.) Above all, <i>melancholy</i> persons give the devil the <i>greatest</i> advantage for raising terrors. That distemper naturally fills men with sad thoughts, and is credulous of the worst evil that can be laid against the one who has it. Of itself it can create the blackest notions and the saddest surmises, and then believes its own fancy. When Satan joins in with this humour, they are the more confirmed in their suspicions, and the terror is the greater, because they are as <i>incredulous</i> of what is good (even when it is told them) as they are ready to <i>believe</i> what is evil, and to believe it <i>because they fear</i> it, though no other reason be offered. But much more so when Satan, in a <i>prophetic</i> manner, foretells their misery and assures them that they must never be happy.
