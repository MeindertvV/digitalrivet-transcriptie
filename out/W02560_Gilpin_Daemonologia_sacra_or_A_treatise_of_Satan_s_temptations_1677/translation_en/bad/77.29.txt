[1] Fourthly, the completing of his method is to please the man in the enjoyment of the peace promised: and this he labours to do, not only to fix the man in his delusion, but to make that man brag of his ease, so as to be a snare to others. And it is easy for the Devil to do this: for, first, the novelty of a new opinion naturally pleases, especially if it gives some seeming credit for discovery or singularity. We see men are fond of their own inventions, and delighted to be lifted up above others. Secondly, Satan can easily allay the storm which he himself raised: he gives over harassing them with anxious thoughts, and on the contrary suggests thoughts of satisfaction. Thirdly, whatever he can do by natural means to raise up our passions of joy and delight, he will be sure to do now, even to rapture and excess if he can; and then he not only makes these men sure (for what argument can stand against such confidence?), but has an active instrument for the allurement of those who cannot discern these methods.
