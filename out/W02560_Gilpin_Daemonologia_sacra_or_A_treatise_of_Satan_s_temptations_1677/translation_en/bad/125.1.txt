[1] 3. Some are of <i>proud</i> tempers, always overvaluing themselves and craving the scorn and contempt of others. This humour is troublesome to everyone about them, but all the trouble finally returns upon themselves. These people think all others ought to honour them and take notice of their supposed excellencies. If men do not, it frets them or stirs up their choler to indignation. <i>Solomon</i>, Prov. 30. 21., in listing the things that greatly disquiet the earth, names <i>a servant when he reigneth, and the handmaid that is heir to her mistress</i>, meaning by this the proud, imperious insolence of those who are unexpectedly raised from a low estate to wealth or honour. <i>He that is of a proud heart stirreth up strife</i>, Prov. 28. 25. As he is troublesome to others, so he creates trouble for himself. He not only torments himself with his own disdainful thoughts while he expresses his scorn towards others (<i>Prov. 21. 24. The haughty scorner deals in proud wrath</i>), but this also provokes the affronts and contempt of others in return, which breed new griefs for his restless mind.
