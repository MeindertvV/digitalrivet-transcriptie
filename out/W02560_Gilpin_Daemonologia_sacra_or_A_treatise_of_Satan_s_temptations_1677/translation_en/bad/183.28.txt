[1] 2. Nevertheless, there is a <i>great difference</i> between God and Satan in this matter, which mainly appears in <i>two</i> things. 1. God does <i>limit</i> himself in all the trouble he gives his children, with his great <i>end</i> of doing them good and healing them in view, and so he <i>restrains</i> himself in the <i>measure</i> and <i>manner</i> of his work, to such a <i>proportion</i> as his wisdom sees will exactly suit his end. So his anger is not like the <i>quarrels</i> of malicious persons, who know <i>no bounds</i>; he will not <i>always chide</i>; his disputes are <i>moderate</i>, and this <i>lest the spirit should fail before him, Isa. 57. 16.</i> So when he wounds the conscience before conversion, it is only to bring people to Christ, and to prevent their taking such courses as might, through delusion, make them stop short of him. As much mourning and fear as is needed for the true accomplishing of this, he appoints for them, and no more. When he wounds after conversion, it is only to let them feel that it is an evil and bitter thing to sin against him, so that their godly <i>sorrow</i> may work <i>repentance</i> suited to the offence, and that they may be sufficiently warned for the time to come, to <i>sin no more</i>, <i>lest a worse thing befall them</i>. He who does not afflict willingly will lay no more grief upon them than is necessary to bring them to this. But Satan, when he is admitted (and God often permits him, in subservience to his own design) to wound the conscience, proceeds according to the <i>boundless fury</i> of his malice, and plainly shows that his desire is to <i>destroy</i> and tread them down, so that they may never rise again. This, though he cannot effect (for God will not suffer him to proceed further than the bringing about his holy and gracious purpose), yet does not prevent his envious thoughts from boiling up in his breast, and he acts according to his <i>own inclination</i>. For it is with Satan as it is with wicked men: if God employs them for the chastisement of his children, they consider not <i>who</i> sets them to work, nor what <i>measures</i> God would probably have them observe; but they propose to themselves <i>more work</i> than God ever marked out for them. Assyria, when employed against Jerusalem, <i>Isa. 10. 7.</i> had designs wider and more cruel than were in God's commission. God had limited him in his holy purpose; yet the <i>Assyrian meant not so</i>, nor did his heart think so, but it was in his heart to destroy and cut off nations not a few. So when God is <i>a little displeased</i> (as he speaks, <i>Zach. 1. 15.</i>), they do all that lies in them to help the affliction forward. Thus the devil endeavours to make things worse for God's children than God ever intended. Here is one difference between God and Satan in the wounding of consciences. <i>2.</i> They are further differentiated in that all that God does in this work is still <i>according to truth</i>. For if he signifies to the unconverted that they are in a state of nature, liable to the damnation of hell unless they accept Christ for salvation on his terms, this is no more than what is true; God does not <i>misrepresent</i> their case to them at <i>that</i> time. Again, if he expresses his displeasure to any of his converted children who have grieved his Spirit by their follies, by setting before them the threatenings of his Word or the examples of his wrath, he does but <i>truly tell them</i> that he is angry with them, and that <i>de jure</i>, according to the <i>rigour</i> of the law and the <i>demerit</i> of their offence, he might justly cast them off; but he does not <i>positively</i> say that <i>de eventu</i>, it shall infallibly be so with them. But Satan, in both these cases, goes a long way <i>further</i>. He plainly affirms to those who are on the way to conversion that God <i>will not</i> pardon their iniquities, that there is <i>no hope</i> for them, that Christ <i>will not</i> accept them, that he <i>never intended</i> the benefit of his sufferings for them. And when the converted provoke God, he does not hesitate to say that the breach <i>cannot</i> be healed, and that they <i>are not</i> yet converted. All these are most <i>false</i> assertions. And although God can <i>make use</i> of Satan's malice, when he abuses his children with his <i>falsehoods</i> to their great fear, to carry on his own ends by it, and to make a <i>greater</i> impression of what he <i>truly witnesseth</i> against them, yet God is not the <i>proper author</i> of Satan's lying, for he does it <i>of his own</i> wicked inclination. The <i>effect</i> of these desperate false conclusions (which is putting his children into fear, in order to his end) may be ascribed to God; but the <i>falsehood</i> of these conclusions is <i>formally</i> Satan's work, and not God's. For he makes use of so much of Satan's <i>wrath</i> as may be to his <i>praise</i>, and the <i>remainder of</i> his <i>wrath</i> he <i>restrains</i>.
