[1] Many things that happen when the mind is in great anxiety can be turned to the accomplishment of this design. For example: (1) Distressing troubles bring the heart into a <i>stupor</i> of amazement. Their thoughts are so broken and disjointed that they cannot unite them into any composed, settled resolution; they can scarcely <i>join</i> them together enough to <i>spell out</i> their <i>distinct</i> desires or endeavours; they hardly know what they are <i>doing</i>, or what they <i>would</i> do. (2) Such distresses also <i>poison</i> the thoughts with harsh notions against God. Great afflictions sometimes make the thoughts recoil against the Holy Lord, with unseemly questionings of his goodness and compassion; and this puts men into a bad, <i>sullen</i> humour of untowardness, from which (through Satan's exploitation) arise the deepest <i>plunges</i> into despair. (3) Most usually in this case the greatest <i>endeavours</i> are fruitless and unsatisfying. Satan, though no friend to duty, <i>unseasonably</i> urges them to repent and pray, but only because they cannot do either with any <i>satisfaction</i>, and then their failures become matter for argument against them. For if they resolve to take up a more severe course of repentance, and accordingly begin to think of their sins, to count them, or to magnify them, they are usually frightened from the undertaking by the hideous appearance of them; they cannot, they dare not, think of them, for the faintest glimpse of them is terrible to a frightened conscience. The <i>raising</i> of them up again in the <i>memory</i> (like a ghost rising from the grave) is far more astonishing than the <i>first prospect</i> of them after the deed was done. So true is that saying of <i>Luther</i>: <i>If a man could see sin perfectly, it would be a perfect hell.</i> If they set themselves to beg pardon by earnest prayer, they are so <i>distracted</i> and confused in prayer that their prayers do not please them; they come away from the duty <i>more</i> wounded than when they began. Or if in some measure they overcome these difficulties, so that they do pray and confess their iniquities, then they urge and <i>force</i> a sorrow or compunction upon themselves, but still with greater <i>dissatisfaction</i>. For it may be (and this usually happens in greater distresses) that they cannot <i>weep</i>, nor force out a tear; or if they do, they still judge that their sorrow is not <i>deep enough</i>, nor in any way suitable to the greatness of their sin. (4) To all these Satan sometimes adds a further load of trouble by injecting <i>blasphemous</i> thoughts. Here he sets the stock, intending to graft upon it afterwards. When all these things are thus in readiness, he comes to set fire to the <i>train</i>, and so he tries to blow up the <i>mine</i>. <i>Is not thy heart hardened to everlasting destruction? How canst thou deny this? Art thou not grown stupid and senseless of all the dangers that lie before thee?</i> Here he insists upon the amazement and confusion of their spirit; and it is very natural for those who are drunk with the terrors of the Almighty to think themselves <i>stupid</i> because of the <i>distraction</i> of their thoughts. I have known several who have pleaded that very argument for this purpose. Satan goes on: <i>What greater evidence can there be of a hardened heart than impenitence? Thou canst not mourn enough? Thou hast not a tear for thy sins, though thou couldst weep enough formerly, upon every petty occasion; nay, thou canst not so much as pray for pardon. Is this not a heart that does not repent, but that cannot repent? Besides</i> (he says) <i>thou knowest the secret thoughts that thy heart is privy to. Do they not boil up in thy breast against God? Art thou not ready to accuse him of dealing thus with thee? What is this untowardness but desperate obduracy?</i> And if with all these there are blasphemous injections, then he tells him it is a clear case that he is judicially hardened, in that <i>he acts the part of the damned in hell already.</i> By all or some of these deceits the devil often prevails so far with men that they conclude their heart to be so obstinate, so stupid, that it is impossible it should ever be softened or brought into a penitent frame, and consequently that there is no hope of their salvation.
