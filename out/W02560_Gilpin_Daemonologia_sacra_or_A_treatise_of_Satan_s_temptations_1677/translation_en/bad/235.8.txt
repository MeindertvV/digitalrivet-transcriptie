[1] To this end he magnifies all their sins. (1.) If he finds them guilty of any <i>great iniquity</i>, he fixes on that and does all he can to make it look as <i>desperate</i> as possible, so that he may call it the <i>sin against the Holy Ghost</i>. In this he has a great advantage, because most men are <i>in the dark</i> about that sin. Not all men yet agree whether it is a <i>distinct species</i> of sin or only a <i>higher degree</i> of wilfulness in some particular sin. On this ground Satan can lay the charge of this sin upon those who <i>apostatize</i> from the truth and through weakness have <i>recanted</i> it. (So he dealt with Spira, with Bilney, with Bainham, and several others.) There is such a close resemblance between the sin of denying the truth and what is said of the unpardonable sin that these men, though they were <i>scholars</i> and men of <i>good abilities</i>, were not able to answer the argument the devil brought against them, and it prevailed to distress them. Satan also has the advantage of fixing this accusation upon others. For whatever the <i>species</i> of the sin may be, if they hold any notion that the sin against the Holy Ghost is a <i>presumptuous act</i> of sin committed under temptation, they will call any <i>notorious crime</i> the sin against the Holy Ghost, because of the more striking aggravating <i>circumstances</i> that have attended such a deed. (2.) He magnifies the sins of God's children by the wilfulness of their sinning. It is often only too true that a child of God may be carried away by a <i>violent impetus</i>, or strong inclination of affection, toward some particular iniquity, where the <i>forwardness</i> of desire in that direction, by a sudden rush, stifles those reluctances of mind which might be expected from one endowed with the Spirit of God, whose power over them ordinarily sways them <i>to lust against the flesh</i>. But it is more common to find a temptation prevailing even though an enlightened mind offers <i>some resistance</i>, which, because it is too feeble, is easily overborne by the strong <i>importunities</i> of Satan working upon the inclinations of the flesh. <i>Both</i> these cases are turned against those about whose state he has got any advantage by making them doubt. If they have resisted only <i>ineffectually</i>, or have <i>not resisted</i> at all, he charges them with the <i>highest</i> wilfulness and so magnifies the matter that they are put in fear, not only that there can be no grace where sin has so much power either to <i>control</i> so much light and endeavour, or has so <i>subjected</i> the heart to its dominion that it can command <i>without</i> contradiction, but also that they can have <i>no hope</i>, that those who sin with so high a hand should ever enter into God's rest. To this end he commonly sets before them that text, Heb. 10.26: <i>If we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins.</i> Or that of Heb. 6.4: <i>It is impossible for those who were once enlightened—if they fall away, to renew them again to repentance.</i> Both passages speak indeed of such a <i>difficulty</i> as, in common speech, is called an <i>impossibility</i>, if not a utterly <i>absolute</i> impossibility of repentance and pardon. But the <i>sinning wilfully</i> or <i>falling away</i> there mentioned is only <i>total apostasy</i>: when men who have embraced the gospel, and by it have received such impressions of power and delight in their hearts, which we usually call <i>common grace</i>, nevertheless reject that gospel as <i>false</i> and fabulous, and rise up against it with scorn and utmost contempt, as Julian the Apostate did. If the true intent of those Scriptures were considered by those who are distressed by them, they would soon see that they were put in fear where <i>there was no such cause</i> for fear. But not all men have this knowledge, nor do they attend so carefully to the matter of the apostle's discourse as to be able to put a right interpretation upon it. Upon such people Satan imposes his deceitful glosses and tells them: <i>Wilful sinners cannot be restored to repentance, but you have sinned wilfully. When sin was before you, you rushed into it without any consideration, like the horse into the battle; or when God stood in your way with commands and advice to the contrary, when your consciences warned you not to commit so great a wickedness, yet you would do it. You were as those who break the yoke and burst the bonds.</i> Granted the supposition that these texts speak of <i>wilful</i> sinning in general, how little can be said against Satan's argument! How many have I known who have been tortured by these texts, judging their state fearful because of their wilfulness in sinning, and who, once the snare of Satan's <i>misrepresentation</i> was broken, have escaped <i>as a bird unto the hill</i>? (3.) When neither of the two former ways will serve his turn, that is, when he meets with people against whom he has nothing of notorious wickedness to object, or people who have a better grasp of Scripture and so cannot be imposed upon in that way, he labours to bring a charge against them from the <i>number</i> of their failings. Here he gathers up all the filth he can and heaps it at their door. It is indeed an easy thing for Satan to set the sins of a child of God <i>in order</i> before him and to bring to mind <i>innumerable</i> evils, especially to one already awakened by a true discovery of the corruption of nature and the vileness of sin. In such a case the more a man considers, the more he will discover, and sins thus set in <i>battle array</i>, though they are not more than ordinarily heinous, yet, being many, have a very <i>dismal appearance</i>. Satan's design here is to bring men under the terrors that seem most proper to be raised from a perverse reading of the <i>third</i> rank of Scriptures, which I pointed out a little earlier. For the Word of God, speaking of the final estate of men, reveals not only the hopeless condition of some as to eternal life because of particular <i>acts</i> of sin, but also the sad estate of others because of the <i>manner, degrees</i>, and <i>frequency</i> of their sinning. The heathen, because they did not improve the knowledge of God that they had from the works of creation, neither drawing those <i>inferences</i> in matters of worship which those discoveries directed them to, nor conducting themselves in full compliance with those rules of <i>virtuous conversation</i> which they might have drawn from these principles, and to which they were bound in point of gratitude, are described thus: Rom. 1.21, <i>They glorified him not as God, neither were thankful</i>; therefore God <i>gave them up to a reprobate mind</i>. And generally, concerning all others, Scripture teaches us that a return to a profane, fleshly life after some reformation carries a greater hazard than usual, as appears from the parable in Matt. 12.45: <i>Seven more wicked spirits re-enter</i>, where <i>one that was cast out is received again</i>, and <i>the last state of that man is worse than the first</i>. So also 2 Pet. 2.20. To the same purpose is that of Solomon concerning the danger of persisting in sin after many reproofs, Prov. 29.1: <i>He that being often reproved hardeneth his neck shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy</i>. These and many such Scriptures Satan has ready, and he plies them home upon the consciences of those troubled with the sense of sin, telling them that their hearts and ways are continually evil, notwithstanding all the courses God has taken to reclaim them; that they have so long neglected so great salvation, or that after seeming to embrace it they became more sinful than before. (They will readily believe this, because they are now more sensible of sin and more observant of their failings than formerly.) <i>There can be no question but they are given up to vile affections; and like the ground that bears nothing but briers and thorns, they are rejected and nigh unto cursing, whose end is to be burned.</i> The wound made with this weapon is not so easily healed as some others already mentioned, because (though Satan unduly wrests these passages to such failures in the children of God as have <i>little or no affinity</i> with them, for they speak only of falling into <i>open profaneness with contumacy</i>) those who have deep convictions, accompanied by great fears, usually think that there are <i>none worse</i> than they are. And though they will grant that some others have <i>more flagitious lives</i>, they think they have <i>hearts</i> so desperately wicked that they must be under as great hazards as those whose lives seem worse.
