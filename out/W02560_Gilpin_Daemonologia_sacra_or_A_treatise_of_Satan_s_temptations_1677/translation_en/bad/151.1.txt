[1] 3. They are also for the most part <i>incessant</i>, and constant troublers once they begin. Although Satan shows <i>variety</i> in the matter of these alarming injections (for sometimes he terrifies one man with blasphemous thoughts, another with atheistic thoughts, and a third with grievous and unusual temptations to sin, such as murder, etc.), yet he usually <i>fixeth</i> his foot upon whatever he <i>first</i> undertakes. Just as cunning huntsmen do not change the game they first start, so that they may more quickly succeed in catching the prey, so Satan persists with whatever frightful thought he begins the trouble with, and pursues it so vehemently that he gives little respite. He makes these unwelcome thoughts haunt them like ghosts, wherever they go and whatever they do. He will make solemn assaults, perhaps <i>twenty</i> or <i>forty</i> times in a day, and he keeps up this rate for what may be a considerable time, so that they are not free of the trouble for several <i>Months</i>, or perhaps <i>Years</i>.
