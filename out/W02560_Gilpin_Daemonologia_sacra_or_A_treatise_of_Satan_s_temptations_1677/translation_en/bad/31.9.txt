[1] But perhaps some may expect higher matters from those who rise above the common rank of men by the reputation of their learning. It cannot be denied that such people have very great advantages for widening their capacities. Yet these advantages do not wholly remove the disorder, and so much incapacity can still be seen in them that the charge of imperfection in knowledge can fairly be brought even against the most learned. Let us bring in some instances, and it will be evident.
