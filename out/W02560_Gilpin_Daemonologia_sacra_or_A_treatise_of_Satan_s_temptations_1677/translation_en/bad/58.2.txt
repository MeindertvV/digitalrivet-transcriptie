[1] First, when he has a design of common or prevailing delusion, he chiefly endeavours to corrupt some person of a stricter, more serious and religious bearing, to be the captain and ringleader; such men were Pelagius, Arius, Socinus, etc. He chiefly seeks to have fit instruments. If he is set on the design of blemishing religion and bringing truth into disesteem, then (as one observes) he persuades into the ministry such men as he foresees are likely to be idle, careless, profane and scandalous; or he endeavours to promote such ministers into more conspicuous places, and provokes them to miscarriage, so that their example may become an objection against truth. Meanwhile he is willing that the opposers of truth should continue their smooth conduct; and then he puts a two-edged sword into the hands of the unstable: can that be truth where there is so much wickedness? and can this be error, where there is so much holiness?
