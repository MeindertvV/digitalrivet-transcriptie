[1] Things that are unwelcome to the listener, or that are said in an unwelcome manner, place, or time, are contrary to the first kind of fitting utterance. For example, when we break into another's speech, we may hinder the course of what the speaker is saying, whether directly or indirectly. Talker and listener ought to take turns, as players do in a game of ball, lest the gathering look like a flock of chattering birds and starlings. <i>Plutarch, On Listening</i>: One must wait, he says, until the other has finished speaking. When he has concluded, one should not contradict him at once, but leave a pause, without adding anything to what he has said if that seems fitting, without changing anything, and without taking anything away. Those who break in straight away neither listen nor are listened to. But one who listens continuously, modestly, and patiently gets from the speaker's words whatever is useful and keeps it, and more easily perceives and detects what is useless and false, and is regarded as a seeker of truth rather than of contention, not as rash or quarrelsome. Envy and spite are the worst assessors and advisers for a listener: they make what is said irksome and unpleasant, turn his judgement awry, snatch away his attention, and wipe out the memory and recollection of what was said. The same Plutarch, <i>On Talkativeness</i>: Let us above all take care that we do not, before the other has finished asking, anticipate him and snatch the answer from him. For it is perhaps not right that, when one person has asked something, we should thrust ourselves in and push him aside. For this seems to insult the one who asked, as if he could not supply what is sought, and to reproach the questioner, as if he did not know from whom he would receive an answer. <i>But such forwardness and rashness in answering especially gives offence</i>. For it at once shows what the one who answered first has anticipated of the question, as if he were saying, "What need is there of that, and what does this fellow know? With me present, no one else should be asked about these things," and so on.
