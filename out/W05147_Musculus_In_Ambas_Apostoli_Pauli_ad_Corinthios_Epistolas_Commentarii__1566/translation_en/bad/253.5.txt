[1] Lyra explains it in his usual way: "If anyone is hungry and cannot wait, let him eat his ordinary food at home, but he is not to receive the Eucharist that same day; he may nevertheless receive it on another day. For the faithful used to communicate together daily in the primitive Church." Thus far he. He held that it was unlawful for anyone to communicate after tasting some food beforehand. We do not praise either a stomach stuffed full or one entirely empty of water, because Christ, the host, delights in neither. As at his supper he feeds the mind rather than the belly, so he seeks not a disposition of the belly but of the mind, by which we may be made capable of heavenly food. What is required is a mind that is fasting and hungry, empty of every kind of unlawful desire: of unbelief, superstition, pride, hatred of the brethren, fornication, simony, and so on.
