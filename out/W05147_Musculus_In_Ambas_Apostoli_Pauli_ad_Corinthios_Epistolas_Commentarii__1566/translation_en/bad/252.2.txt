[1] "Alius," he says, "wait for another." Note again that he does not command them to abstain from the Lord's Supper, but warns them to correct its abuse, since they gather to eat. He does not say, "Therefore from now on do not gather to eat," but, "Therefore when you come together to eat, wait for one another." He does not prescribe how long they must wait, or whether, if one or two are missing, the whole congregation must abstain from the Lord's Supper. He simply says, "Wait for one another," which should not be narrowed scrupulously to waiting for the arrival of this or that absent person; it is to be understood as a correction of those who, as soon as they had come to the place of assembly, presumed to eat their own private meals without waiting for the crowd. He says, "Wait for one another," as the remedy for this abuse. It is usually irksome for those who are ready to wait for those who are unready and somewhat slow. Yet such is the condition of society, not only of the church but of any fellowship whatever, that it requires the patience of waiting for one another; Saul, refusing this patience when he did not wait for Samuel's arrival, involved himself in a grievous sin.
