[1] Notice, however, that he does not say, "For God and the Father must wait," or "For he must act patiently until, all enemies having been overcome, he can hand over the kingdom complete and entire," but "For he must reign." Therefore it is not through the weakness of either the Father or the Son that all enemies are not destroyed at once and the kingdom is not brought to completion. It comes from a certain arrangement, by which the Son must reign, and must subdue his enemies beneath his feet not by enduring them until they perish of their own accord, but by reigning, that is, by the divine power. Hence what is read in Hebrews 10, "henceforth waiting until they are made his footstool," must be read with caution. For we do not have Christ resting and waiting in heaven, idle or powerless, but reigning, and not only in heaven among the angels but also on earth among all his enemies, according to the saying of the Psalm already cited: "Rule in the midst of your enemies." Among their number was Julian the Apostate, who, when he was being destroyed, cried out, "You have conquered, Galilean." All who until now have been unwilling to kiss the Son and serve him, but have preferred to oppose him, lie prostrate beneath his feet, and they will not escape the power of the reigning Christ, as many as his enemies and foes still remain until the end of the world. For who will cast down from the throne him whom it is necessary that he reign, and that for so long until he emerges the conqueror of all his enemies? Let this saying of the Apostle, "Christ must reign," be fixed in the memory of all the godly, so that they may console themselves against all afflictions, temptations and persecutions with the firm faith in the reigning Christ. His kingdom is indeed hidden from the eyes of the world, because it is not of this world but heavenly. Yet his power rules over tyrants and all enemies, who, although they may seem to reign for a time and to be about to utterly extinguish Christ's people, are themselves in truth destroyed and extinguished. This becomes evident at the end, when the outcome of events proves it.
