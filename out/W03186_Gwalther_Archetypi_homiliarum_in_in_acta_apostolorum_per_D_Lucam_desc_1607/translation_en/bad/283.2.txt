[1] After Saint Stephen has set out the origin of the Jewish people down to their descent into Egypt, he now goes on to describe their remarkable increase, their oppression and their deliverance. He takes particular care over this, because these things are a type of the common redemption made in Christ. So the aim of these events is the same as that of the earlier ones: to show that the Jews were preserved and multiplied not through the law and the legal ceremonies, but by God's free mercy, according to his promises. From this he concludes that the worship of God and our salvation depend on faith, because we lay hold of his grace and promises. Let us therefore look at the individual points in order.

[2] I. He shows the cause of all things, namely God's grace and faithfulness, who willed to keep his promises: "When the time drew near," etc.

[3] But this promise was made before Abraham had sons (Gen. 22.15–17, etc.). Therefore it was free, so that nothing can be attributed either to the dignity or to the merits of the forefathers. By the same argument we are taught that we too are saved by grace (Eph. 1; Rom. 9, etc.).

[4] NOTE. He assigns a fixed time to the promise, which is expressed in Gen. 15: "In the fourth generation," etc. The genealogy agrees: from Jacob, Levi, Kohath, Amram, Moses (Exod. 6). Therefore God's providence governs not only the sum of things but also each individual circumstance, since it extends to the very smallest matters (Matt. 6.10), and likewise to the appointed moments of time (Acts 1). Similar passages are Jer. 25.29 and Dan. 9. This is a comfort if he sometimes delays help in adversity. Let us remember that the time is predetermined, and that he will not neglect it. It is ours to wait, etc. (Ps. 62). Those who prescribe fixed limits to God tempt him (Judith 8); see also Num. 11 and Ps. 78. Therefore always keep in mind that saying of Hab. 2, and Matt. 6. This applies privately in sicknesses, and publicly in the persecutions of the Church, when everything seems about to collapse (Rev. 6).

[5] § 3. Finally, exposed, he is saved through the daughter of Pharaoh, who adopts him. Thence he is brought up and instructed in all wisdom, etc. Yet this could have wholly corrupted Moses. That he afterwards came to true faith and became a servant of God, he owed to God's free mercy, who does not allow his own to be snatched from him (John 10).

[6] God therefore marvellously mocks tyrants (Ps. 2). Pharaoh nurses in his own bosom, educates and trains the avenger of the people whom he wished to see oppressed. So Saul (with) Jonathan; Ahab (with) Obadiah (1 Kings 18); Herod (Matt. 2); Saul (Acts 9.8). The same has often happened with emperors. And today too they sometimes advance the Gospel, though they wish it extinguished. Let us therefore trust in God, and pray.
