[1] The will of a King is very numinous; it has a kind of vast universality in it, and is many times greater than the will of his whole kingdom, stuffed with ill counsel and ill precedents. If it is not a foot and a half less than the will of his Council, and three foot less than the will of his Parliament, it is too big. I think it would be well for a King if he had no will at all, but were all reason. What if he committed his moral will to Divines who were not Bishops, his political will to his Parliament and a Council chosen by Parliament, so that if ever it miscarried they might blame themselves most and him least? I scarce know any King who has such advantage as ours; his three kingdoms lie so distinct and entire that, if he pleases, he might keep them like three gardens without a weed, if he would let God keep his will, without wilfulness and rashness.
