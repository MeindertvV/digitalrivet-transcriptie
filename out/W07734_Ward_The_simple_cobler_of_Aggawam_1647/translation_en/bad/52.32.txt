[1] To speak more plainly, I have wondered these thirty years what ails Kings. In my time I have seen the best part of twenty Christian Kings and Princes, and yet, Christian as they were, one or another of them was always scuffling for prerogatives. It must be granted on all hands that <i>the prerogatives of the king</i> are necessary supports of the state, and stately things suit stately Kings. But if they are at the same time <i>derogatory to the realm</i>, they are little things to wise Kings. Equity is as due to the people as eminence to princes, and liberty to subjects as royalty to kings. If they cannot walk together lovingly, hand in hand, <i>side by side</i>, they must cut the girdles and part as good friends as they can. Nor should it be taken offensively that when Kings are hauling up their topgallant, subjects lay hold of their flaplines. The head and the body must move alike. It is not fitting for me to say with Horace, <i>As you bear your fortune, Caesar, so we will bear you.</i>
