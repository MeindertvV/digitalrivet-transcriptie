[1] Authority must have the power to make and keep people honest, and people must have honesty enough to obey authority. Both need a joint council to keep each safe. Moral laws, royal prerogatives and popular liberties are not of man's making or giving but God's. Man is only to measure them out by God's rule, and where man's wisdom cannot reach, man's experience must make up the difference. These essentials must not be Ephorized or Tribuned by the discretion of one man or a few, but must be sanctioned in due succession by supreme councils. For <i>pro-re-nascent</i> occurrences, which cannot be foreseen, diets, parliaments, senates or accountable commissions must have power to consult and act against intersilient dangers and flagitious crimes forbidden by the light of nature. Yet it would be good if states let the people know this much beforehand, by some carefully framed <i>manifesto</i>, so that gross offenders could not tell tales of anchors and buoys, nor excuse their presumptions by pleading ignorance. I see no difference in these essentials between monarchies, aristocracies or democracies. The rule and the reason will be found to be the same, whatever the schoolmen and the praetorians may say. And in every case the best standard for measuring prerogatives is the plough-staff, and for measuring liberties the sceptre. If the terms were altered a little, into loyal prerogatives and royal liberties, we should be sure to have royal kings and loyal subjects.
