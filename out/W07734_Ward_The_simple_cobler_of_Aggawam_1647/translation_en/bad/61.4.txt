[1] Is it not a most unworthy thing for you to run away from your Subjects in a day of battle, when on their pikes you could come through safe, with your bare breast and welcome? Is it honourable for you to be fleeing on horseback from men who would count it their greatest honour to carry you on their humble shoulders to your Chair of Estate, and set you down on a cushion stuffed with their hearts? Is it prudent of you to be enraged with your best friends for risking their lives to rescue you from your worst enemies? Were I a King (pardon the supposition), I would hang that Subject by the head who would not take me by the heels and drag me to my Court when he saw me scrambling for my life in the ruined countryside, if nothing else would do it. And I would honour the very heels of those who took me by the very head and taught me, by all just means, to rule better when they saw me unkinging myself and my kingdom. Do you not know, Sir, that just as, when your people are sick of the king's evil, God has given you a gift to heal them, so, when you yourself are sick of it, God has given the Parliament a gift to heal you? Has your subjects' love been so great to you that you will spend it all and leave your children little or none? Are you so exasperated against wise <i>Scotland</i> that you will make <i>England</i> your fool or footstool? Has your father's son become more orthodox than his most orthodox father, when he told his son that a king is for a kingdom, and not a kingdom for a king? This is parallel to that of the Apostle: the husband is not without the wife, nor the wife without the husband.
